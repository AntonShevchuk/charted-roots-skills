# Bessarabia, Moldova, the Dniester region and the Romanian layer

Read this file when a family is connected with Bessarabia, Moldova, the MASSR, or the Dniester uyezds of Podolia and Kherson guberniyas. The tables set out search routes; they do not guarantee survival or access. The mark "check" flags a detail not confirmed for a specific place: verify it against the inventory or the record while continuing other useful checks.

## 1. Jurisdictions by period

| Period | Territory | Authority and division | What changes for the search |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1812–1873 | Bessarabian Oblast | Russian Empire; uyezds (in early documents, «цинуты» / ținuturi), volosts, parishes | Orthodox metric books of the Kishinev Ecclesiastical Consistory; revision lists |
| 1856–1878 | Southern Bessarabia (Cahul, Izmail, Bolgrad) | Principality of Moldavia, then Romania | A separate jurisdiction inside the "Bessarabian" period; check language and institution from the book |
| 1873–1917 | Bessarabia Guberniya | Russian Empire; uyezds: Akkerman, Beltsy, Bender, Izmail, Kishinev, Orgeev, Soroki, Khotin | Orthodox books usually on Russian printed forms; check confession, calendar and file composition separately |
| 1918–1940, 1941–1944 | Bessarabia | Kingdom of Romania; județ, plasă, comună | Romanian forms in Latin script, civil registration; calendar — section 3 |
| 1924–1940 | MASSR (left bank of the Dniester) | Autonomy within the Ukrainian SSR on the territory of the former Balta uyezd of Podolia Guberniya and the Tiraspol and Ananiev uyezds of Kherson Guberniya (check composition village by village) | Soviet ZAGS; Cyrillic and Latin script in different years (section 3); capital Balta, from 1929 Tiraspol |
| 1940–1941, 1944–1991 | Moldavian SSR and Ukrainian SSR | In 1940 the northern part of Khotin uyezd went to the Ukrainian SSR (Chernivtsi Oblast), Akkerman and Izmail uyezds to the Ukrainian SSR (Izmail Oblast, from 1954 part of Odesa Oblast); the western part of the MASSR joined the Moldavian SSR | The successor archive depends on the raion, not on the word "Bessarabia" |
| 1941–1944 | Transnistria | Romanian occupation administration between the Dniester and the Bug (parts of Podolia and the Kherson region) | Romanian documents on territory that before and after was the Ukrainian SSR |

For each event record the jurisdiction on the event date (`SKILL.md`, Workflow step 2). One village may appear in documents under a Russian, a Romanian and a Soviet name; record the found forms in the Place note's `aliases:` once the place is identified. Look for a hamlet without its own church through the established parish, which may have been in another village.

## 2. The Dniester zone

Where migration or family ties are established, consider both banks of the Dniester. The table lists directions to check, not exact neighbourhood boundaries for each period:

| Bessarabian uyezd | Uyezds to check across the Dniester | Candidate archives |
|---|---|---|
| Khotin | Kamenets, Ushitsa, Mogilev uyezds of Podolia Guberniya | For Khotin uyezd — ANA and the Chernivtsi oblast archive (check); for Podolia — the archives of Khmelnytskyi and Vinnytsia oblasts |
| Soroki | Yampol, Olgopol uyezds of Podolia Guberniya | ANA; Vinnytsia oblast archive |
| Orgeev, Bender | Balta uyezd of Podolia Guberniya; Tiraspol and Ananiev uyezds of Kherson Guberniya | ANA; for Kherson Guberniya — the State Archive of Odesa Oblast |
| Akkerman, Izmail | South of Kherson Guberniya | ANA; the Izmail archive (now within the Odesa archive system — check) |

For Tiraspol uyezd ANA publishes ф. 211, оп. 23 «Tiraspol». This is not full coverage of the uyezd and not proof that all parishes listed belonged to one diocese. Check the historical diocese and the creating institution of the specific book; for parishes of the Kherson diocese also consider the State Archive of Odesa Oblast and the actual holder of the book. An empty inventory in one archive does not exclude a village or a record.

Kamenets-Podolsky Oblast (1937–1954) was renamed Khmelnytskyi Oblast in 1954. Later publications (memory books, databases) often give the post-war name instead of the one stated in a 1940s document.

Where a Ukrainian archival reference code is available, Duck Inspector (`seed-sources.md`) may help find an online copy; judge its applicability by the current coverage of the specific fond, not just by the guberniya name.

## 3. Language, script and calendar

- **Imperial period:** Orthodox books are often in Russian with pre-reform orthography; ф. 211 also contains other confessions, languages and recording forms. Determine the language from the document itself.
- **Romanian administration 1918–1940 and 1941–1944:** expect Romanian and Latin script, checking the specific book; kinship formulae — `field-lessons.md`, section 2.
- **MASSR and Moldavian SSR:** allow for script changes. The MASSR used Latin script in 1932–1938, then returned to Cyrillic; in the Moldavian SSR the switch to Latin script came in 1989. Establish the language of the institution and the script of the specific record separately.
- **Calendar:** do not determine it from the authority or language alone. Check the confession, the book's title, double dates and neighbouring entries; civil and church practice may have differed. Record the date as written in the transcription; put the ISO value on the Event and note the calendar in the Source body (if unclear, say the calendar is unknown). Conversion and date conflicts — by `ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, section 5, and `habsburg-galicia-bukovina.md`, section 3.

Guides for query variants, not rules for correcting the source text: ă — «э» (scribes also wrote «а», «е»), â/î — «ы» (also «и»), ș — «ш», ț — «ц», ce/ci — «че/чи», ge/gi — «дже/джи», che/chi — «ке/ки», ghe/ghi — «ге/ги», j — «ж», -ui — «-уй». Russian scribes added the endings -ев/-ов, mixed voiceless and voiced consonants (д/т) and wrote names down by ear.

Test a form like «Иван Петров» both as a possible short patronymic and as a surname; distinguishing them by context — `field-lessons.md`, section 2. In queries use reasoned full and short forms, without assigning a father on the strength of an ending alone.

A scribe may have written a second surname or nickname («Х, он же Y» — "X, also known as Y"). Keep both forms as written and check the second surname in other years of the same book; it is a lead, not proof that all bearers of the second surname are one family.

How to read a file:

1. **Establish the file's composition from the inventory.** One volume may combine several parishes for a year, but this is not a universal arrangement: in inventory 23 for 1911 there are separate files by settlement. Use the title, dates and list of parishes/folios of the specific file; do not infer them from the uyezd and year alone.
2. **First find the parish's section.** A FamilySearch frame number is not a folio number. Open the frame with the title page of the relevant book («Метрическая книга … церкви села … округа … уезда на … год» — "Metric book of the … church of the village of … , okrug …, uyezd …, for the year …") and note the frame-to-folio correspondence. Do not read entries of a neighbouring parish as those of the target parish.
3. **Determine which parts survive:** births/baptisms, marriages, deaths/burials. If there is an annual summary, reconcile what you read against it, distinguishing acts, people and pages (`field-lessons.md`, section 1). A missing part or summary does not devalue the available entries.
4. **Attached hamlets** are found in the book of the established parish, which may have covered several villages. The place of residence in an entry distinguishes residents of the village from those of the hamlet; find the hamlet through the inventory and the title page of its parish's book.
5. **Check coverage by year.** Match the available years and parts of a book against the ANA inventory. A gap on FamilySearch does not mean the book is lost: check with the holder and, if needed, draft a request under the existing rules for writing to the archive (`correspondence.md`; draft in the letters folder, the user sends).

Record a sweep of a book or year range — including nil results — as a `Survey -` note with its coverage (opis, delo, years, parts, frames read).

For an inheritance act you can start with the alphabetical index or recording book for the relevant year. Match the date and act number found there against the register and the inventory. For example, in a hypothetical inventory the old index «7/14» is repeated on two storage units. Until checked against the register, keep both leads. After checking, cite the current archival designation and keep the old index separately per `search-result-validation.md`, section "Catalogue description and movement of documents". Check the heirs' names and relationships against the act itself.

On 07.11.2026 the official descriptions of the four fonds and selectively extracted text of the inventories of ф. 1437 and Р-3014 were read. ANA warns of propaganda vocabulary in Soviet-period inventories. Use the headings to find storage units, and check the information stated there against the acts and other sources. Access to an individual act and the conditions for obtaining a copy — per [ANA's working procedure](https://arhiva.gov.md/sala-de-studiu/) and `correspondence.md`.
