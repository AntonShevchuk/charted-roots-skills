# Field lessons: unindexed scans, repression and military databases, vanished villages

Read this file when research runs into unindexed scans, repression and deportation records, military databases, places no longer on the map, or when open sources are exhausted. These are practical techniques from research into various family lines. No personal data from files is included here; examples are illustrative.

Web lookups, portal searches and downloads described below need the user's explicit instruction for the task (`SKILL.md` § Rules that win). Everything that ends up in the vault goes through `add-archive-record` (transcription + Source + scan in the archive folder, Citations, Events); coverage and nil results go into the `Survey -` note.

## 1. Unindexed scan collections

Most metric books and civil acts of the former USSR on FamilySearch and archive portals are published **as images only**. A name search does not find them; they have to be read.

### Selective preservation of evidence

Before leaving a page, save the material findings, disputed potentially relevant entries and the material on which an important exclusion of a candidate rests. For plainly unrelated entries a coverage note in the Survey is usually enough. First check whether a usable copy is already in the vault (the archive folder, by archival code and by name); do not save a whole collection for the sake of individual findings.

Prefer the image or document file the source offers for permitted download; otherwise a legible capture made with a permitted tool, with enough context. Check that the file opens, that its format matches its content, that the relevant spot is legible and that the image matches the frame or other locator. If saving fails, record the reason and the return point in the Survey or Search Plan and continue with other available checks; do not present mere viewing as a saved copy. Vault scans are `.jpg` or `.pdf` only, named per `archive.md` § Scan files; working fragments and the received originals stay in the session scratchpad.

**Downloading on FamilySearch.** Open the needed image on its own, not from the thumbnail grid. Check whether the standard Download button is available for that particular image, even if it was disabled in another group of the same collection. If the button is available and copying is permitted, wait for the right frame to load and check that the file obtained opens and matches it. Saving a link to your account does not create a file. If the viewer reports a contractual restriction, record the locator and what could be read. For a copy, check the permitted route with the custodian. A screenshot must not stand in for copying that is not permitted. See the [FamilySearch help article on downloading](https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/how-do-i-download-historical-record-images).

Before a large download or extraction, estimate the space needed, including derivatives, and choose a manageable targeted batch. Access restrictions and rights to onward transfer or publication still apply; being able to view does not extend them.

### Finding the right film or file

1. Search the catalogue by place, using **the historical names and languages of that place and period**. One village might be named differently in the Empire, in interwar Poland or Romania, and in the USSR. On FamilySearch, per a contributor's observation of 29.10.2026, the catalogue place search did not include subordinate places, while Explore Images by guberniya showed image groups for its uyezds and villages; one settlement was sometimes set up under three or four place variants (Russian Empire, USSR, modern country). Check the place and period variants relevant to the question. The author of [#184](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/184) reported on 01–02.11.2026 that in the mirror of Ukrainian inventories (описи) they examined, the Russian form of a name did not find a line written in Ukrainian. With such a gap, choose spellings justified by the catalogue's language and the history of the place; the observation does not establish the language of every mirror and does not require trying every form.
2. If the archival reference (fond, opis, delo) is known, use services that map references to online copies. For Ukrainian archives this is, for example, Duck Inspector (`inspector.duckarchive.com`): given a reference it shows the FamilySearch film number (DGS). On `429`, stop requests to that source and proceed per `browser-and-captcha.md`, section 7.
3. For a film found, open the image-group summary (on FamilySearch, the Explore Images page): place, date span and record type give a bearing for the search. The author of [#184](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/184) reported on 01–02.11.2026 that the Information panel of the film they examined also listed the items with their references and years; use such a breakdown, where available, as an index, checking it against the title pages. If the summary, a nested unit and the actual content contradict each other, check the title page and the relevant section; the procedure for resolving description conflicts is in `search-result-validation.md`. One image group (DGS) may contain several files with different references, separated by target frames. Per a contributor's observation of 30.10.2026, the first target of a group named one file, while a target in its middle named the next one, with its own title page; earlier extracts had listed the whole group under the first reference. For a finding, record the file designation from the nearest preceding target and title page, and the item number if given. The number on a target and on the certifying sheet may belong to an earlier numbering. On a discrepancy, check the heading, place, record type and years against the custodian's current inventory. For an order use the confirmed catalogue reference, keeping the target's designation for citing the copy. If the correspondence is unclear, keep both references on the Source and clarify the reference with the custodian.
4. Record the "holes" too: which years or record types are missing online. Without this a negative search means nothing.
5. **A parish's files under another name.** The author of [#147](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/147) reported on 01.11.2026 that the headings of a parish's books used the village's second or later name, and a filter by the first name missed them. If the service supports search within document text, search for the question-relevant forms of the village and parish name inside the entries, group the hits by file and check their headings. Match what you find against the known books by place, years and record type before treating a coverage gap as confirmed; a similar name by itself does not establish the same parish.

On «Поколения Пермского края» (Generations of Perm Krai) the author of [#40](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/issues/40) reported on 27.10.2026 books without a full name index and with an inconsistent order of years. This is an observation about individual books: check the title page, section and frame range of the relevant file. A direct image address, or a pattern verified against the viewer, may be used for permitted local reading of the needed frames of the chosen file, respecting access rules and rate limits. Check that the image obtained belongs to the right file and frame; do not treat the path format as a stable API.

The author of [#182](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/182) reported on 07.11.2026 that in one composite file the leaves of deaths lay among the leaves of births and marriages of the same parish and year. If the part you need seems incomplete, check the headings, columns and summary leaves in the question-relevant range. The position of a frame by itself does not establish the part of the book. A confirmed number sequence and totals help reveal breaks, per "Numbering, dates and book totals" below. In the Survey coverage, state the parts actually viewed and the unchecked stretches, keeping an unknown cause of a gap as unknown. Establish a numerical share of survival and physical loss only from additional information on the composition of the file.

### Numbering, dates and book totals

If a book has sequential numbers or summary totals, use them to check the tally of entries viewed. This is an additional control when working through a section, not a mandatory recount of the whole book for the sake of one finding.

- **Establish the unit of counting.** A line, an act, a person and an event do not always coincide. Twins may share a parents' line and have two numbers by sex; a repeat photograph of the same act does not add a person. Check separate male and female sequences within their own sections, allowing for corrections, gaps and restarted numbering.
- **Separate the dates.** The month of the section, the date of birth and the date of baptism can differ; so can death and burial. A birth on 31 May and a baptism on 1 June is admissible in the June section. If the birth month is not written and has been inferred from the sequence of entries, mark it as an inference from context; one position within a section is not enough. The year range of the whole book does not establish the exact year of a single undated fragment.
- **Year of the section.** The author of [#147](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/147) reported on 01.11.2026 that OCR of a handwritten year on a printed form gave contradictory readings. For a disputed entry, check the image of the heading of the relevant year section and the entry's position in the sequence of acts, allowing for breaks and restarted numbering. A viewer label is a bearing, not a substitute for the image. Do not use an unverified OCR year for dating or for correcting an age; if the heading is unclear, keep the uncertainty.
- **Month of the section.** If only the day is written next to an act, check the nearest preceding frames for a month heading. Match the heading to the act by leaves, numbers and dates. If numbering is separate, check the relevant series. Where a monthly total or the next month's heading exists, check on the image where the boundary falls. From the columns and neighbouring acts, determine the month of the event in question, allowing for a possible crossover between birth and baptism. If the link between heading and act, or the month of the event, stays unclear, keep the uncertainty.
- **Keep three results apart:** the total written by the clerk, the arithmetic sum of its columns, and the number of entries actually counted. On a discrepancy, check page carry-overs, repeat shots, twins, inserts and corrections in the affected stretch. For example, a written 85 when the columns sum 35 + 32 + 16 = 83 is kept as 85 with a remark; do not add people or change their sex or estate to make it match.
- **Limit the conclusion.** Matching totals support the tally check for the stretch viewed, but do not confirm each surname and do not prove completeness of the paper file or the digital collection. The grand total can match despite errors in subgroups; keep an unresolved discrepancy next to the coverage in the Survey, without holding up conclusions that are already well grounded.

- **Order of books in a composite volume of copies.** Per the observation of the author of [#160](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/160) of 02.11.2026, in one volume the books followed the official names of the churches on the title pages, not the everyday names of the villages. If the order is unclear, check several title pages, the meaning of the corner number and a possible table of contents. Narrow the range you need only after checking the order.
- **Annual alphabetical list.** If there is an index at the end of the year, use it to reach the act. Check the particulars against the entry itself. A name missing from an incomplete index leaves open whether the act exists, so for a material gap choose a suitable stretch to read.
- **Annual summary of deaths by age.** If the book has such a table, use it to choose years and entries in a long search. Check which section it covers, and take neighbouring age bands into account if the age is approximate. State the years, groups and entries read, and the gaps. The age may be inaccurate, and entries by group may diverge from the summary even when the grand total is right. If a discrepancy or gap matters for the question, consider reading the corresponding section. A negative conclusion applies to the entries selected.

### How to read

For searching a metric book and reading an entry in detail, use `metric-books-scans.md`: choose the columns by the task, check candidates against the full image, and record the coverage viewed separately (Survey). If a film has text indexes, use `familysearch-indexes.md` to discover candidates; check the material fields and the unindexed frames against the images.

1. **The form changes** with a change of regime and decade: columns shift, the language of the entry changes (Church Slavonic → Ukrainian → Romanian → Russian). Recalibrate the column coordinates on the first page of each new form.
2. **Book copies get jumbled.** The left half of an entry (dates, child's name) and the right half (parents) sometimes end up on different frames. Look for notes such as "see attached sheet" (Rom. *vezi coala alăturată*) and supplementary inserts (*copie-completare*) out of order. Blank and duplicate frames are normal; look at neighbouring frames before concluding.
3. **Later marginal notes.** Keep the full text of the annotation, its position and its relation to the main entry. Do not infer the kind of action, the applicant or the reason for the application from single words such as «выслано» (sent) or «повторно» (duplicate): check the formula, the particulars and the available context. If the note plainly records the issue or dispatch of a certificate, treat that action as a separate fact. By itself it does not establish whether the person in the main entry was alive, who applied, or how they were related to that person. Distinguish applicant, recipient, request date and issue date; add them only as far as written. Keep an annotation of unclear purpose as an open question in the Hypothesis or Search Plan; do not turn an administrative date into a life event (no Event for it).
4. **A word lost in the gutter.** The author of [#150](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/150) reported on 02.11.2026 that a father's surname illegible at the binding in a birth entry was clarified from the death entry of what was presumably the same child. Match dates, parents, place and other distinguishing features; the same name does not establish identity. In the transcription, do not present a restoration from another document as visible text of the original line (mark it in `## Uncertain readings`).

**Metric books of the transition years.** The author of a report on the books of one parish observed the formulas «гражданин» (citizen), «гражданка» (citizen, fem.) and «гражданская жена» (civil wife). Establish from the column and neighbouring entries whether the word serves as a status designation. On the single word «гражданская», leave the form of marriage unknown. Compare the full formula with local usage. Check the form and date of the marriage against suitable marriage documents. [Observation by the author of #246](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/issues/246)

For the transition period, check the actual years and sections of the church and civil series against the opis and the book. Take into account the historical jurisdiction on the event date and the institution that made the entry. Keep a rank or position verbatim, and check service and membership of an army against military documents. Establish the date style per `ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, section 5.

### Single-column overview strips

When hundreds of frames of the same kind must be checked for the sake of one column — the parents' or groom's column in a metric book, the line «Фамилия, имя и отчество домохозяина» (surname, first name and patronymic of the householder) on a 1917 census household card — cut the same area out of each frame and stack the strips into contact sheets with the frame number at each strip; choose the batch size for legibility, e.g. 12–24 frames: `python scripts/scan_montage.py <folder> --out <strips-folder> --box x0,y0,x1,y1 [--half auto] [--rotate -90] [--first N --last M] [--contrast 1.4]`. The area is given as fractions of the frame after rotation; first tune it on a few frames and check that the surname is not cut off where a line wraps, then go through the strips for the chosen range. The script needs Pillow; use it if it suits the environment, or existing image tools otherwise. Keep the originals separately: the script does not overwrite existing files and reports ambiguous frame numbers as an error. Its output goes to the session scratchpad, never into the vault or the skill folder. Open candidates in full at full resolution: a strip serves the search, not the reading of the entry. Note frames where the column is covered by an insert or illegible, and repeat shots of the same card. If the census form chosen names only the householder, the absence of a surname among householders does not exclude kinship through the female line and does not prove the family's absence from the settlement if some volumes are not digitised.

The order of a file does not always follow settlements. Per a contributor's observation of 29–30.10.2026, in the files of the X revision (1857–1858) examined for one uyezd, the «сказки» (revision lists) were bound by date of submission, settlements mixed, no index, and the title of each list named the settlement, the owner and the date. In such a file, a strip of header bands (e.g. every other frame, with a closer look wherever the settlement changes between adjacent samples) helps find the right lists without reading every frame; sampling every other frame may miss a short list, and unviewed frames stay in the Survey coverage as unviewed.

The script prints the path of each finished sheet once to standard output, and sends the names of skipped images without a frame number, and the resume hint, to standard error. If it fails while building a sheet, the finished sheets stay in place. If the script names the next frame, fix the cause and resume from that `--first`, keeping the other parameters the same for the series. A sheet's filename gives its first and last frame numbers. Check which frames went in against the source folder, and in the Survey coverage mark the stretches actually read, per `research-coordination.md`.

For extended image viewing use small batches and compact results per `research-coordination.md`, section "Extended scan reading". Do not bring all images into the main context.

### Eighteenth- and nineteenth-century revision lists without surnames

Yu. A. Kobyakov (Ю. А. Кобяков) gives an example of a peasant recorded without a surname in a revision list of 1816. In a formula of the type `first name + patronymic form + сын (son)`, establish the role of the patronymic form from the grammar, the column and neighbouring entries. For searching by the father's name and later forms see `ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, section 3. [«Воронежский вестник архивиста» (Voronezh Archivist's Bulletin), 2010, issue 8, pp. 83–84](https://arsvo.ru/images/publikacii/vestnik_arhivista_8_dlya_interneta.pdf#page=84).

Per the observation of the author of [#130](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/130) of 31.10–01.11.2026, in the files of the I–III revisions examined, peasants are often recorded by first name and patronymic only, without surnames, so a surname search does not find them. This is an observation about particular files, not a full survey of the early revisions. Useful techniques:

- **Chain back from later revisions.** Start with a revision where the family is already identified and match the previous one by the column of age «по прошлой ревизии» (at the last revision), the composition and ages of family members, notes on movements, and family numbers. Do not treat the family number as a permanent identifier across revisions. At each step check the settlement, the administrative department and the owner of the part of the village: parts of a village with several owners may be in different books; check a possible change of department, including palace and appanage («дворцовое», «удельное»), against the opis and documents. A step without a surname is identified by the whole cluster of features, allowing for errors and age discrepancies; that is an inference, not a surname read — say so in the Hypothesis. If the features admit several families, keep separate candidates.
- **The village's second name.** A village might be recorded under another name with the note «тож» (also called) («Село А, Б тож»), and sit in the file's alphabetical index under the first name, which is absent from later gazetteers. Search under both names; a negative result under one of them does not close the search.
- **Stan and parish.** In the witness statements of clergy of 1723 about their parishioners examined by a contributor in files of the I revision, the uyezd, «стан» (rural district), village and church are given. If such statements survive for the relevant area, check the stan and parish of the village against them.
- **Indexes of departed persons.** At low magnification the settlement name in an index is often illegible. Look for neighbouring entries marked «того ж села» (of the same village): establish from the structure of the index which heading the note refers to, then open the corresponding article of the book and check the settlement name against its heading at full size.
- **Numbers in indexes.** The number at an entry may be the number of the list, not of the leaf, and the columns differ between files (e.g. leaf and number of souls, or list number and leaf). Read the column headings and test the value on one article before searching by numbers.

### Confession lists

- **Composition and order.** Establish the purpose of the file from its heading, the composition of its sections and the internal index, if any. In the opis, documents on the clergy or separate lists of those who did not come to confession may sit next to the «исповедная роспись» (confession list). Determine the coverage from the file itself. In a composite volume, check the division by deaneries («благочиния») or other groups and their numbering. Use the parish's position in another year's volume as a bearing. Matching the opis against the cover and title page is described in `search-result-validation.md`, section "Catalogue description and movement of documents".
- **Household and parish.** Record the church, year, settlement and group under which the household is listed. This gives a route to the metric books for that place and period. If the village had several churches or the attachment changed, check the question-relevant sections. Describe absence from one section with that section's actual coverage.
- **Kinship in the household formula.** Keep the original formula and check from the lines and columns whom the kinship words refer to. The formula «вдова [name of the deceased]» (widow of …) tells you about the woman's husband. The formula «дети её» (her children) ties the children to the named woman; paternity is established from separate evidence. If a clear formula names the woman's children by a first marriage, keep that as the list's statement. On a discrepancy with another year's list, check whether these are the same people and lines, and compare with birth, baptism and marriage entries as the question requires. Check material readings per "Checking a visual reading" below.
- **Copies and the age column.** If lists repeat the composition and wording, check their origin and dependence per `evidence-protocol.md`, section "Independence". Changed numbering helps spot a different order, but the origin of the list is established separately. If an age shift is suspected, check the household boundaries, column lines and neighbouring rows. Leave a doubtful match open.

### Premarital investigations and questionnaires

Besides the marriage register, parishes kept documents checking for impediments to marriage: «брачные обыски» (premarital investigations), questionnaires, *examen sponsorum*. Depending on the confession and period they name the parents of the groom and bride, age or year of birth, estate, residence, marital status, whether the parents are alive, and the dates of betrothal and banns. Such documents often form a separate series and may survive where the marriage register is lost or not digitised.

- Look for them in the opis of the parish, deanery or «деканат» (Catholic deanery) next to the metric books; check the columns against the specific form.
- An investigation records the intention and the parties' statements, not the wedding itself. Check that the marriage took place, and its date, against the marriage register or later entries in which the couple are named as spouses.
- Establish where the information on parents and age came from: the parties' statements, an attached metric extract or another record. Borrowed information does not become independent confirmation (`evidence-protocol.md`, section "Independence"). Use a note «родители умерли» (parents deceased) as a time bound only for identified persons and the date of the statement.
- If the names stand in the heading of the leaf, a header strip is suitable for the search ("Single-column overview strips" above); read the leaf found in full.

### Alphabetical ZAGS journals of the 1920s–1930s

Some sets include alphabetical journals (indexes) to the act books. They help find the act number when the book itself is unavailable; an index entry does not replace reading the act. Before searching, establish how the particular set is organised:

- **Check volumes and departments.** One year may be split by months, departments or restored acts. From the cover, the opis and the headings, record the question-relevant range and gaps in the Survey; absence from one volume does not cover the others.
- **Establish the order within a letter.** If entries really are arranged by date of registration, a known period helps narrow the reading. Read the column names, check ditto marks and the sequence of act numbers; do not assume chronology or continuous numbering for the whole set.
- **Registration date is not birth date.** Registration lagged birth by days and weeks; read a range after the birth date, not just the same day.
- **Check another surname only on grounds.** If the material points to the mother's or stepfather's surname or another form, search for it under the corresponding letter for a suitable period; there is no requirement to go through all neighbouring letters.
- **Namesakes in the same department** can be a lead to the family circle, but do not establish kinship; identity is settled per `SKILL.md` workflow step 6 (Identify).
- **State a negative result with its coverage:** department, volume, letter, date range and quality of reading of the pages (Survey, `result: negative` if the sweep is nil). Keep an illegible line as unresolved, not as the absence of the person sought.

### ZAGS act books of 1919–1940s

The author of [#204](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/204) described on 30–31.10.2026 examples from books of Poltava and Sumy oblasts (`seed-sources.md`). Check the structure and coverage of the book you need from its content.

- **Territory of the book.** Under the heading of a village council, acts of neighbouring villages and khutors of the former volost may sit. If the settlement's name is not found, check the books of the former volost centre, the volost executive committee («волисполком») or the district ZAGS bureau by historical subordination. The table of contents, the cover and the entries themselves help establish which settlements and act types went into the book.
- **Year of event and year of registration.** In the names of some PDFs the years referred to events registered later or restored. Do not determine the registration date from the range in the filename alone. Check both dates against the entry and look for notes that acts continue in another book.
- **Columns of the form.** In the birth entries examined there were particulars on the parents' cohabitation, the order of the marriage and the number of children born and living. The length of cohabitation helps estimate when it began. A year of marriage registration needs its own basis in the entry. In death entries the declarant could be an institution; check the declarant's relationship from the text. Read a ditto mark within its own column (see below).

### Checking a visual reading

Apply this to handwritten tables and fragments where the reading affects a research conclusion. For a clear entry a targeted cross-check is enough; repeat passes are needed for a specific doubt, not for their own sake.

- **Structure of the entry first.** Distinguish visible lines, columns, headings, the boundaries of a family group and annotations. If a heading is cut off, name the columns provisionally by position; do not substitute the expected document type. If necessary, open the full page or neighbouring entries and note what was taken from that context.
- **Preserve the layout of the text.** Do not merge people from different lines, numbers from neighbouring columns, or later notes with the main text. Distinguish an empty cell, illegible text and a ditto mark. If it is unclear whom an annotation refers to, keep it separately without assigning it to a person.
- **File size and viewing conditions.** A tool may show a large original downscaled. If the letters are too small, open a separate crop of the original and match it to the full line or page. Keep the crop's link to its source: ID/path and the area coordinates in the source's pixels. Ordinary enlargement creates no new detail, and the file size does not prove the resolution the model actually received; if that is unknown, say so. Generatively filled-in strokes may not be used as evidence of what the original says.
- **Targeted reading by a person.** If an illegible spot matters for the conclusion, you may show the user the fragment together with the full line and samples of the hand, while continuing other checks. Use the available image tools and keep the original: processing can create visual artefacts, so the reading is checked against the unenhanced fragment. Record the reader, the source, the coordinates and the doubts in the transcription's `## Uncertain readings` section (`archive.md`), per `evidence-protocol.md`; another reading of the same document does not become an independent chain. One opinion, including a negative one, does not automatically remove an unresolved contradiction.
- **Kinship words in cursive.** An error in a short formula («снъ» (son, abbreviated), «з синами» (with sons), «зять» (son-in-law), «братъ» (brother), «вдова» (widow)) or in a name tied to it can change the type and direction of a relationship. The author of [#197](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/197) reported on 01.11.2026 that the model missed «снъ» and took «з синами» for «зять» although the neighbouring text was clear; a targeted check against a large fragment corrected the reading. Check a formula and names material to the conclusion in the context of the household, in the order described above; a separate crop is needed if the scale prevents distinguishing the strokes. Leave an unresolved reading and relationship disputed.
- **Month in Roman numerals.** With two readings, e.g. IX and X, compare the disputed stroke with other dates in the same hand, if available. A filename and an extract carried over from the first reading belong to the same chain. Check the bounds against dated records relating to the same event or to an established sequence. A departure note may give a lower bound for an arrival, and a record of an arrival that has already happened an upper bound. If the bounds admit both readings, keep both dates with their grounds. If one outweighs the other, state the more probable version. In the vault, carry the uncertainty in `## Uncertain readings` and on the Event (a broader `date` with matching `date_precision`, plus a note) rather than silently picking one month.
- **A confident repetition does not confirm a reading.** If the answer does not match the visible lines, columns or numbers, check the image actually opened and the disputed stretch. A matching SHA-256 confirms the file, not the correctness of the transcription or the working of the viewing channel. A model's explanation of individual letters must also be checked against the image. Keep the earlier reading, the correction and its basis (in `## Uncertain readings`; the git history keeps the prior version); if the contradiction is not resolved, leave the spot illegible or disputed rather than repeating the request merely to get the model to agree with itself.
- **Method and context of a re-reading.** For navigation and first-pass work choose a fast, economical available method if its accuracy suffices for the task. For a disputed reading, a contradiction or a material identification — when available, when delegation is permitted and the budget suits — you may bring in a more capable independent reader or model; such a check is not required for every clear entry. For an unprompted reading, pass the image, the locator and a neutral question without the presumed answer. When checking an identification, pass the question-relevant documents and contradictions: independence from someone else's conclusion does not require hiding necessary facts. Note whether the checker saw the earlier transcription, the presumed names and additional pages; do not call a useful comparison made with hints an unprompted reading. Check the versions against the source: a reading without the earlier answer does not guarantee accuracy, and a re-reading does not create an independent source (`evidence-protocol.md`, section 6). With a single model or no check available, continue with explicit uncertainty. Poor image quality cannot be fixed by choice of model: consider another permitted copy or targeted help from a person.

## 2. Features that help link records

- **Exact date of birth in a marriage entry.** Many forms have columns for the "age, day, month and year of birth" of groom and bride. A match of exact birth date and house number helps match people; first distinguish the baptism date from the birth date. For corroboration, check where the information came from: if the date in the marriage entry was copied from the metric record, it is one chain for the birth date. A shared parish by itself does not establish dependence or independence of the entries; evaluate the information about the marriage itself separately. Application to claims: `evidence-protocol.md`, section 6.
- **House number.** Check the stability of numbering for the particular place and period: houses were renumbered, several families and generations might live in one house, and a yard number in a list might be merely ordinal. A matching number helps match households only where the numbering system is established; check identity and kinship by the cluster of parents, dates, godparents and other features.
- **Stillborn and deceased infants** are recorded in the birth books («мертворожд.» (stillborn), Rom. *mort născut*). Take them into account when reconstructing a family.
- **Kinship formulas you need to be able to read:**
  - Ukrainian/Galician possessive form: «Іван Петрів», «Марія Іванова» may denote the father, but Петрів or Іванова may also be surnames. Determine the meaning from the column heading, the grammar and neighbouring entries; do not assign a parent from the ending alone;
  - Romanian (Bukovina, Bessarabia 1918–1940): *fiul lui X* / *fiica lui X* — son/daughter of X; *a lui X* — "(son/daughter) of X"; *n.* or *născută* — née;
  - Church Slavonic and Russian: «сынъ» (son), «дщерь» (daughter) denote the relationship. Read «Родомъ X» (by origin X) in context: it may be a locality, an estate or a registration, not a maiden name. Check a presumed surname against an explicit «урождённая» (née) formula and other family entries; keep the ambiguity as a hypothesis;
  - German: *Sohn des*, *Tochter des*, *geb.*;
  - Polish (Catholic metric books of Poland, Lithuania, Belarus, Latgale): *z Kowalskich*, *z X-ów* after the name of a mother or wife may denote the family/maiden surname. Distinguish it from a place by grammar, column and other entries. The endings *-ówna*, *-anka* were used in surname forms of daughters, *-owa* for married women or widows; the endings themselves do not prove a specific relationship. Read *po Janie* in the full formula: e.g. *wdowa po Janie* refers to the widow of the late Jan ([WSJP PAN](https://wsjp.pl/haslo/podglad/4568/wdowa/1218864/kobieta)), not automatically to a father or grandfather. *młodzian* — young man, *panna* — maiden; establish marital status from the full formula; *pracowity*, *włościanin* may denote rural social status — check the meaning against the form;
  - Latin (17th–18th centuries): *filius/filia N. et M.* — son/daughter of N. and M.; *conjugum legitimorum* — of lawful spouses; *levantes*, *de fonte* may refer to godparents in the baptism formula. Expand the abbreviations *LL.*, *C. L.* from the headings, full text and local practice, not automatically. *de X* may refer to a place or to origin; determine which word the phrase attaches to. *subditi Ecclesiae N.* — subjects of the church of N., with no automatic inference about occupation or kinship. *spurius*, *illegitimus* denote illegitimate birth, while *parentum ignotorum* means unknown parents; these are different facts. The note *Hic multa metricae desunt ob vetustatem libri* reports omissions due to the book's condition and limits a negative conclusion.
- **Death entry listing surviving relatives.** Some forms list the surviving spouse and children. The list helps match the family to births or marriages but does not replace checking identity and the origin of the information. A child's absence from the list does not by itself refute kinship or prove death: the completeness and criterion of the list are unknown. If the discrepancy matters for the question, keep the alternative explanations in the Hypothesis and choose a distinguishing check per `evidence-protocol.md`.
- **Godparents and sureties as leads to kin.** Keep the role, date and place named in the act. A godparent from another settlement may suggest where to look for records of the mother's family, including an earlier generation. Particulars of the bride's surety lead to his own acts, in which the bride or her kin may be named. An entry recording someone's participation in a baptism or marriage may contain a surname omitted from that person's other acts. Check the reading of the name against the relevant records.
  - Matching patronymics, reciprocal godparenthood and a recurring circle of sureties give a testable hypothesis. Read a directly stated relationship in its full formula and check it against those people's own records. Assessing features and independence — per `evidence-protocol.md`; distinguishing civil witnesses from godparents — per `ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, section 7. Searching by the bride's parish is described in section 8.
  - Estimate an approximate birth window from the age in the act and other records. Take participation rules into account if they are established for that place and time. The role by itself does not establish age or the fact of a marriage. If an earlier record calls the godparent a child of both of the infant's parents, it may widen the window for searching that couple's marriage. Check the date of the marriage separately.
- **Name traps.** Георгий (Georgy) and Григорий (Grigory) are different names; Romanian *Gheorghe* and *Grigorie* are likewise not interchangeable. Юрий (Yury) and Егор (Yegor) are historically related to Георгий, Гриць (Hryts) is a form of Григорий; in documents Юрий, Егор and Георгий may be independent names. Use well-founded forms as search variants (`aliases:` once established), not as a ready identity of people. On a discrepancy, first check namesakes by other features; treat a clerk's error as a hypothesis and keep the conflict as a separate claim in the Hypothesis note.
- **Index quirks.** In some census and metric indexes the patronymic is recorded in the surname field («Иван | Петров Сидоренко»). An exact surname search then finds nothing: search more broadly and filter the results locally.

For a presumed conversion from the Old Belief or another confession, also check the records of reception into Orthodoxy and chrismation: they may name adults and their ages, even when they sit in the "births" part. Establish the event type and date from the text; do not turn the date of the rite into a date of birth.

## 3. Repression and deportation (USSR, 1940s–1950s)

- **Regional publications of archival documents** are one of the best routes. For example, projects publishing documents of the SBU sectoral archive, or the volumes of «Літопис УПА. Нова серія» (Chronicle of the UPA. New Series). They can contain:
  - lists of families for deportation with household composition, years of birth, the number of the registration file, and an «основание» (grounds) column (the head of household's sentence, or «пособник» (accomplice));
  - texts of books of the rehabilitated;
  - lists of criminal case numbers in the archive;
  - reports of the underground and of the MGB with a village-by-village chronicle: raids, arrests, confiscations of property.
- **The 1944 deportation of the Chechens.** Lists of the rehabilitated of the MVD of the Chechen Republic and their mirrors, a shared rehabilitation date as a weak household indicator, renamed villages, and the custodians of special-settler registration files are described in `chechnya-deportation.md`.
- **Books of the rehabilitated** often group people by village council or post-war district, not by the actual village. Check the pages of neighbouring villages.
- **Russia: rehabilitation and access to files.** Requests about rehabilitation and the fate of the repressed, access to files, and searching for documents on dekulakisation — `russia-records-access.md`, sections 2–3.
- **Possible co-defendants in one case.** If the place and date of the decision are known, search suitable cards of «Открытый список» (Open List) for people with the same body and date of decision. Compare the case references, and confirm a shared proceeding from archival documents. Records of exile from the same district may suggest a direction for the search. A matching date, district or surname does not by itself establish kinship, a shared case, or the place of exile of the person sought.
- **Built-in search on small sites** often does not work. Crawl only the needed section (a few hundred pages, one at a time, with pauses), save locally to the scratchpad and search the text. If a site returns errors to non-browser clients, open the pages in the built-in browser rather than spoofing the User-Agent.
- **Context for interpretation (check against the case document every time):**
  - in 1947–1950 there was no death penalty; 25 years was the maximum sentence;
  - "participation in the OUN" was usually charged under art. 54-1 "a" of the Criminal Code of the Ukrainian SSR (in the RSFSR, 58-1);
  - families were deported by decisions of the Special Board of the MGB «как членов семьи участника ОУН» (as family members of an OUN participant) or as «пособников» (accomplices);
  - mass deportations were sometimes framed as a «ответная мера» (retaliatory measure) for a specific event in the village. Link the family to the operation, not to the event, unless the document explicitly states the link.
- **Access (Ukraine).** The 2015 law on access to the archives of the repressive organs opens files for inspection. Prepare a draft request giving the archive, fond, file number, full name, year and place of birth — drafted in the letters folder in the recipient's language, never sent by the agent (`letters.md`, `correspondence.md`). Check the current address and procedure on the official site; sending rules are in `privacy-and-safety.md`, section 9.
- **«Сидел(а) при Сталине» ("was imprisoned under Stalin") is not the name of an article.** A family report of a conviction in that period does not establish the legal qualification or the political character of the case. A negative search in databases of the repressed does not establish the article and does not exclude a conviction: record the coverage actually checked in the Survey. From the known place, time and kind of record, identify the custodian — it may be a departmental archive or an information centre of the MVD, including the ГИАЦ (GIATs, Main Information and Analytical Centre of the MVD of Russia). Check the existence of a file, the institution's competence, the right of access and the documents required against the current official procedure; drafting the request — per `correspondence.md` (letters folder, never sent by the agent). Do not extend the specific observation of the author of [#150](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/150) to the composition of all repression indexes.
- **Family retellings "drift" over time.** A family version "died in the camp" can diverge from a document recording a fixed-term sentence and later release. Record both versions, each with its source (oral history as its own Source), in the Hypothesis; do not pick one.

## 4. Military databases

- **OBD «Мемориал» (`obd-memorial.ru`)** — documents on losses and personnel, including those who came back from the war. Its [advanced search](https://obd-memorial.ru/html/advanced-search.htm) includes conscription and demobilisation lists, military transit points and reserve regiments, nominal rolls of units, the officers' and naval card files. As of 27.10.2026 the default HTML form ticks the main loss categories; Books of Memory and personnel are not ticked. Choose sections according to the task and record the filters actually applied in the Survey. The absence of an entry proves neither absence of service, nor death, nor survival.
  - **Books of Memory.** The author of [#259](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/259) reported on 12.11.2026 a discrepancy between the viewer's frame counter and the printed pagination. For an entry found, keep the link to the image and the visible locator, and give the printed reference by title, volume and page of the book if they are legible.
  - To obtain a file, use the document link the source offers, or open the available image in the viewer. Check the saving conditions for the specific material.
  - Advanced search supports a substring of the place of birth (`pb=T~<text>`). The «Книги памяти» (Books of Memory) toggle adds `book=1`.
  - Check the applied conditions against the form and the content of the cards. A count that equals a search by surname alone is a reason to check the place filter, but does not by itself prove a failure. The author of [#50](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/50) reported that on 26.10.2026 the choice of document groups in the advanced form affected the result; choose the group set according to the task, with no universal parameter set.
  - **Control query.** The author of [#259](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/259) reported on 12.11.2026 that a hand-built quick-search address could return an empty result although records were available. In that case repeat the search through the standard form. The author of [#146](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/146) reported an empty result when passing `pb=T~<substring>` through the page address. Test a new or doubtful query method on a known record per `search-result-validation.md`, "Control query" (section "What question the sample answers"); if the result is not confirmed, record the incompleteness and try the standard form. This does not require repeating the control for every search already verified.
  - The author of #50 also observed changes of order and gaps when paging. Record the volume viewed and the IDs available; if you counted unique cards, compare their number with the counter. For a key person a targeted query is useful. An incomplete pass does not prove the absence of an entry.
  - Places are abbreviated: «х.» — khutor (hamlet), «с/с» — village council, truncated endings.
  - **"Surname nest".** In a relevant result set, group the entries by the place given, distinguishing place of birth, residence and conscription. Allow for repeats of one document, several documents about one person, and separate namesakes. The cluster shows the distribution of bearers of the surname who made it into the chosen database and helps choose where to search next; it does not establish kinship or the family's place of origin.
- **The female line through a gravestone and relatives in military records.** The surname on a gravestone refers to the moment stated in the inscription and is not necessarily the maiden name. Read the photograph per `browser-and-captcha.md`, section 3, separating confident from conjectural readings. If the mother and the child's patronymic are known, the mother's surname and the first name implied by the patronymic help search for a candidate father or spouse in a military database; such a query does not by itself establish kinship.
  - Read the «родственники» (relatives) and «адрес семьи» (family address) columns from the image: the index may not show them. The designation «жена» (wife) is a direct statement of the relationship in this source, but not a marriage registration act and not proof of parentage for the next generation. Check each edge separately.
  - Neighbouring graves and a shared enclosure are a lead, but do not prove kinship. Keep alternative candidates and conflicts, including differing patronymics, in the Hypothesis.
  - Read the event date in the text of the document. Distinguish the date of the event, the date of a quoted letter, the date of an order and a processing stamp: the index may have taken any of them for the date of leaving service. Read a ditto «то же» (the same) from the preceding entry in the same column, allowing for page breaks. If the unit's subordination differs between the date of the battle and the date of the report, keep both statements with their dates and check the unit's documents.
  - If it is established that a Book of Memory or a repeat list reproduces the same entry, treat them as one archival item — one Source, not corroboration (`archive.md`: one Source per archival item); check independent origin or dependence for the specific documents, not by the title of the publication.
- **«Память народа» (Memory of the People) and «Подвиг народа» (Feat of the People).** On an actual CAPTCHA proceed per `browser-and-captcha.md`, section 5: one permissible attempt in the internal browser, then if necessary open the relevant tab for the user and hand over the exact query, continuing with available sources.
  - After a successful check in the built-in browser, use the available legitimate session: go through the question-relevant documents of the group. A repeat check may appear on a new search.
  - **Strong match in the results.** If the full name and the available features such as year of birth agree, open the available card and the linked document, even when the snippet shows an unfamiliar town. From the field headings determine whether the town refers to birth, conscription, service or another event. Keep the candidate and the discrepancies until checked against independent features. If the card is unavailable, record the lead and the limit of the check.
  - **Individual cards and groups.** A combined album may hold fewer fields than an individual card. Open the needed cards and images where available. If an expected document is missing, check suitable search variants and the [ungrouped results](https://pamyat-naroda.ru/how-to-search/), if that setting is available. Match documents of different groups by date and place of birth, unit and other features per `evidence-protocol.md`; keep uncertain links as hypotheses. If a card offers a «Похожие документы» (similar documents) block, check the question-relevant links. Establish the link to the candidate from the document itself.
  - On an award sheet look at the **reverse** too: it holds the resolutions of the division, corps and army commanders and the final note on the order. A downgrade of the award is visible only there.
  - **Masked fields.** Per a contributor's observation of 30.10.2026, on the image of an award sheet in «Память народа» the field «Постоянный домашний адрес» (permanent home address) was covered by a grey block. Do not treat a masked field as empty; look for the family address in the loss report, the service record card, or by a request to TsAMO quoting the sheet's reference.
  - In the «Документы частей» (unit documents) section of «Память народа», per a contributor's observation of 30.10.2026, the date filter from the page address was not applied: choose dates in the form and check against the results.
  - **Long-service awards.** In 1944–1957 the medal «За боевые заслуги» (For Combat Service), the Order of the Red Star, the Order of the Red Banner and the Order of Lenin were also given for length of service (10, 15, 20 and 25 years). The decree heading «за выслугу лет» (for long service) distinguishes them from combat awards. The military ID does not distinguish these cases.
  - **Military commissariat records at the place of registration.** The author of [#91](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/91) reported on 30.10.2026 entries with a military commissariat, a conscription date and a «дата выбытия» (date of removal). Keep the field name and the kind of registration: removal may relate to death, a move or other deregistration. Without an established reason it does not set an upper bound for life or a year of death. Record a difference between the commissariat and the family's place of residence as a discrepancy, not as a proven move.
  - **Place of birth on card-file cards.** If the short result list of «Подвиг народа» does not show the place of birth for a fitting candidate, check the available card: the field may be inside it. Absence of the field in the result list does not exclude the candidate.
  - **Search by place of birth.** Per the observation of the author of #91 of 30.10.2026, the advanced search of «Память народа» accepted part of a place-of-birth name together with a surname and returned similar surnames. Check the filter actually applied and the content of the cards per the procedure above; a match of place and surname is a lead, not a kinship link.
  - **Place of birth from the suggestion list.** The authors of [#146](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/146) and [#148](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/148) reported that choosing a place suggestion created `place_birth_ids`, while plain input into `place_birth` in their checks could change the order without narrowing the results. These are observations of particular queries, not a guaranteed API. Check the area chosen, the conditions actually applied, the counter and suitable cards; distinguish districts of the same name. An unchanged counter by itself proves neither that the filter applied nor that it failed. The author of [#240](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/240) also reported narrowing by part of a name. On a discrepancy, check the actual coverage per `search-result-validation.md`, "Control query" (section "What question the sample answers").
  - **Year of birth and place of conscription.** The author of [#240](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/240) reported that in the queries checked a year range changed the order of the results, while the commissariat filter missed entries with no place of conscription or with a different spelling of the district. Check the filter's effect against the cards and a control query. For a material gap, try suitable query variants without the doubtful restriction.
  - **Card without an image.** If the interface reports that the document is unavailable, keep the archival reference, the named custodian and the actual message. The [portal's help](https://pamyat-naroda.ru/faq/) describes unpublished images and refers to the archive. Logging in again makes sense only when authorisation is explicitly required. Clarifying access with the custodian — per `correspondence.md` (draft in the letters folder).
  - The award-sheet questionnaire field «участие в Отечественной войне с …» (participation in the Patriotic War since …) shows from when the person considered themselves at the front; rear service (military schools, reserve units) is usually not included.
  - **Irrecoverable-loss report: relatives' columns.** Per the observation of the author of [#143](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/143) of 03.11.2026, in the nominal list of form No. 2 they examined, columns 10–11 held particulars of relatives and an address not shown by the search card. Check the headings and the image of the specific list. «Родных нет» (no relatives) conveys the content of the entry, not proven absence of relatives; assess each relationship separately.
  - **Wound card file.** Per the observation of the author of [#143](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/143) of 03.11.2026, some wound cards showed a hospital, a discharge date and a reference to the Филиал ЦАМО (TsAMO branch, military medical documents) without an image. Absence of an image does not mean absence of the original. Keep the available reference and ask the named custodian whether a copy is possible; the request is drafted per `correspondence.md`.
  - **Differing places of conscription.** When a loss report, a Book of Memory and a commissariat record disagree, keep each version with its source and check identity per `evidence-protocol.md`. A reprint of the same entry belongs to the original evidential chain (one Source). If there are signs of pre-war service, the pre-war place of residence gives a lead to the conscription documents.
  - **Saving the image.** The author of [#143](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/143) reported on 03.11.2026 a direct-download error while the viewer worked. In that case use permitted selective saving per section 1 and `browser-and-captcha.md`, section 3; the failure of one channel does not permit circumventing access.
- **Place of birth in different documents.** An award sheet may name a village, while a registration card names a larger administrative unit. Compare the original spelling and the column headings on the images. Establish which place the index carried over, and whether village and district correspond in the relevant period (`ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, section 1). Distinguish birth, conscription, registration and family address.
- **Destination on demobilisation.** The place given is a lead to post-war registration and to documents on residence. Check actual arrival and residence against later records. The custodian of the list helps reach the document itself.
- **Missing-in-action record and a later demobilisation.** Keep both records and check whether they refer to one person. Establish the time of the event and the origin of each report. A unit's report and commissariat information given by the family may reflect different circumstances. Once identity is established, compare the events in time. Keep the cause of the discrepancy as unknown until the sources explain it.
- **Pension annotation on a death notice.** Read its wording. If it indicates that particulars were passed on for pension registration, it is a lead to the corresponding file. Check award, payment and kinship against the documents concerning them. Establish custodian and access for the specific place and period.
- **Burial documents in the hero's group.** Per the observation of the author of [#150](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/150) of 03.11.2026, besides the loss report the group held a burial list, and the page `/memorial/burial/<id>/` gave the ВМЦ (military memorial centre) number, places of reburial, dates and numbers buried. If relevant to the question, compare the available documents on the original burial and the reburial, keeping them as separate events and the original spelling of places. A matching or similar name does not establish the same settlement; check the content of the fields and the origin of coordinates on the specific card, and do not treat the path as a stable API.
  - In the [burial passport and nominal list](https://obd-memorial.ru/html/sources.htm), check whether other people from the same report, killed in the same place on the same day, are included, if that helps explain a gap. A group omission is a lead to clarify with the custodian. One omission does not establish the actual place of burial or the transfer of remains.
- **Battle context.** A historian's lecture with a timecode may give a lead to a combat log or a reference. Check automatic subtitles against the recording of the lecture, and ground conclusions about the person in his documents.
- **«Дорога памяти» (Road of Memory).** The [museum](https://1418museum.ru/faq/) accepts photographs and particulars from users. Check the origin of the photograph and of each statement separately. With incompatible particulars, keep the candidates apart until checked. Respond to an access barrier per `browser-and-captcha.md`, section 7. The author of [#260](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/260) reported on 13.11.2026 that the place of birth was not shown in his results. If the features are insufficient, open the cards of suitable candidates and compare the particulars available. The coverage of a query by place remains unestablished.
- **District lists of war participants on administration websites.** Per the observation of the author of [#150](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/150) of 02.11.2026, a municipal list included returned war participants with years of life and settlements. Such publications can give class-B leads (`seed-sources.md`, section "Classes") and surname spelling variants, but check completeness, composition and origin for the specific list; they are not a roll of all families of the village. Match a find against a document. Record a certificate or access failure as a limitation of this route, not a negative result.
- **Diminutive name in a document and in search.** Per the observation of the author of [#150](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/150) of 03.11.2026, a query with a diminutive name returned cards with the full form. This does not establish universal normalisation or that exact search is impossible. Check the original spelling in the document against the image; distinguish name variants and identity of people per section 2.
- **Family memorial pages.** In a real case, an extract from a service record was found on a family page of «Бессмертный полк» (Immortal Regiment) after the report was "complete", and it closed three open questions. That is the reason to include such pages in the first pass: search route — `source-strategy.md`, section 12; evaluating text and scans — `evidence-protocol.md`, section "Originality".
- **Place of birth in foreign POW databases.** German and Finnish indexes (`seed-sources.md`, sections "Military sources" and "Finland") contain transliterations and historical administrative names: `Gebiet`/`Bezirk`, `alue`/`kaupunki` help recognise the level of description but do not establish the place by themselves. Keep the original spelling and distinguish the date of the event, the date the record was made, and the period the geographic designation refers to. Check the place against the district and a gazetteer of the relevant period (`ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, section 1); use German and Finnish surname variants in a targeted way per section 2. A similar-sounding name remains a hypothesis until checked.
- **Austro-Hungarian casualty lists 1914–1919** (CompGen index, `des.genealogy.net`): search by surname and home place (*Heimatort*); gives regiment, company, year of birth and status (wounded, captured, killed). Useful for Galicia and Bukovina, for men born roughly 1860–1900. First names and surnames there are in Polish and German transcription: *Hryć* — Гриць (Hryts), *Wasyl* — Василий (Vasily), the ending *-czuk* instead of «-чук» (-chuk), and so on.
- **Memorials of the war since 2014** (e.g. `memorybook.org.ua`) — leads only, to modern namesakes.

## 5. Places: vanished hamlets and inaccessible territories

- **A place of birth that no longer exists.** If a passport records a settlement absent from current gazetteers, OSM and Wikipedia, it may be a khutor (hamlet) abolished in the 1950s–1970s. Check:
  - the "place of birth" fields in military documents of the 1940s (old khutors appear there);
  - old topographic maps;
  - handbooks of administrative-territorial division (for the Ukrainian SSR, as of 1 September 1946 and the editions of the 1960s–1980s);
  - neighbouring village councils.
- **Villages in reservoir zones.** If a known village is missing from the modern map, check the relevant lists of vanished settlements, flood zones and information on resettlement. Compare maps from before and after the change of territory, and administrative handbooks. A later record may give the place of resettlement; do not substitute it for the place of birth without reading the record itself.
- **Districts and oblasts were often redrawn in the 1940s–1950s.** Record the jurisdiction as given in the document. In the vault, a place missing from the gazetteer is a stop-and-ask point (`SKILL.md` § Rules that win); historic chains can be anachronistic — a schema decision, not a fix (`CLAUDE.md`).
- **Burial and origin.** The place of a grave does not establish the place of birth or of the family's origin; check these events separately.
- **Open reports of funeral agencies.** Cards of completed burials sometimes contain gravestone photographs and coordinates. If it helps the question, look for the card of a known deceased relative; earlier neighbouring monuments may appear in the photo. With the coordinates available you can check, in a targeted way, cards relating to the same plot. Coverage depends on the firm and the period it worked, not on the whole history of the cemetery; an example catalogue is in `seed-sources.md`, section "Maps, churches and cemeteries".
  - Classify the photograph of a legible gravestone as an image of the original (`image-of-original`; a cemetery `source_type` from `customSourceTypes` if the vault registers one, else ask); evaluate the card text and the civil death record separately. Check dates and identity against other suitable sources; proximity and a shared enclosure do not by themselves establish kinship (this file, section 4).
  - Evaluate dependence by the origin of the information for the specific claim (`evidence-protocol.md`, section 6). Repeat photographs of one monument do not create independent confirmation of its inscription (one Source); a shared website does not by itself merge different underlying records into one chain.
  - Keep in the vault only the information and fragments needed for the question, noting the source URL, the access date and the crop area. If a crop suffices to read and identify the monument, keep it instead of the full funeral photograph; minimisation of data on living people and any version for sharing follow `privacy-and-safety.md`, sections 1–4 and 8. Record a change in the page's availability as a limitation of the source.
- **"Born" and "grew up" are different claims.** Family memory often names the village where the person grew up. Keep these claims separate, each with its own source. Checking that a place existed on the event date, finding the parish through a neighbouring uyezd, and geographical leads — `ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, section 1.
- **Occupied and war-damaged territories.** Local archives (village councils, district ZAGS offices) may be inaccessible or lost. State this limitation and direct requests to oblast and national institutions.
  - **The Russian database «Без срока давности» (No Statute of Limitations).** Choose the question-relevant cards by place and period (see `seed-sources.md`). Text from the card helps find the relevant passage. The author of [#223](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/223) reported on 11.11.2026 that a link labelled «.doc» returned a DOCX file. Check the format by content, and a material reading against the image. Keep the card's address, the archival reference and the locator of the passage read. Separate editorial headings about people and events from what the document says. Assess the transcription, image and reprint of the same document as one chain of origin (`evidence-protocol.md`, section 6) — one Source.

## 6. Family materials as sources

For preparing a conversation about a specific question, use the optional prompts in `relatives-interview.md`; choose topics by the task and by the answers already known.

- **Passport:**
  - marriage stamp (spouse's surname at the time of marriage);
  - residence registrations («прописки»);
  - place of birth as written;
  - «особые отметки» (special notes);
  - the children page (only for minors as of the date of issue).
- **Children's dates of birth and the parents' marriage registration date** help reconstruct the chronology. Report them neutrally, without judgement.
- **Audio recordings of family accounts** (`source_type: oral_history`):
  - transcription with timecodes;
  - uncertain words listed in `## Uncertain readings`, not marked inline in the transcript;
  - every claim flagged as oral;
  - comparison with documents in a separate table in the Hypothesis note.
- **Leading questions and prompting.** Start with an open question, and keep the answer about a candidate proposed by the researcher separately, with the context of the question. Do not turn "could have been" into an independent recollection or an upgrade of the relationship's status. Note possible prompts from earlier questions, documents and material published about the case; assess the origin of the specific item per `evidence-protocol.md`, section "Independence". The absence of a detail from the prompts does not by itself prove independence, and access to one page does not by itself devalue all independent recollections. Use an oral rendering of a surname as a search variant, checking it against suitable local documents, without automatically correcting the original spelling.
- **Stepfather and stepmother in the vault.** Marriage to a parent does not by itself establish parenthood of that parent's child: record the established marriage (spouse slots) without automatically adding the step-parent to the child's `father`/`mother` or the step-parent's `children`. Distinguish biological, legal and household relationships in the Person's `# Research Notes` and the Hypothesis (`evidence-protocol.md`, "Adoptive, guardianship, step and household relationships"); do not substitute a proven adoption for either biological kinship or mere marriage. Keep a version of "which marriage the child is from" based only on years of birth as a hypothesis until a distinguishing check.
- **Family origin story.** Keep the original wording, the source of the story and the branch it may concern. A surname ending such as «-ский» (-sky) does not by itself determine geography or ethnicity. Start from identified people and move to earlier records along confirmed links. If the story does not yet fit one line, consider other branches or known places of residence and service, when there are grounds. Check the time and place of a migration against the records concerning it.
- **Notes, diaries and letters provided for the research** are oral or derivative sources. Give the date of writing: "per a note of …".

## 7. Technical techniques in the built-in browser

- **Deep-zoom viewers.** Assemble only the needed fragment from tiles instead of downloading the frame at full resolution.
  - For quick viewing, join the same column from 4–8 frames into one image.
  - View a suspicious line separately at full resolution.
  - Working fragments go to the scratchpad; an evidential scan kept in the vault takes the transcription's filename, with any archival code verbatim in front (`archive.md` § Scan files), not an ad hoc date-event-name pattern.
  - Use the share of "ink" pixels only to pre-select presumably blank pages. Check the image before excluding it: a faint entry or note may fail the threshold.
- **Zoom levels.** Per the observation of the author of [#247](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/247) of 12.11.2026, a larger mode of the ОГАЧО (OGAChO, United State Archive of Chelyabinsk Oblast) viewer could enlarge the leaf without adding detail. If it matters for the reading, compare the strokes of one fragment at a comparable size through the standard zoom. Choose a sufficiently legible permitted image, keeping its link to the frame. File size by itself does not show how much detail is available.
- **Faded and water-damaged leaves.** If the viewer offers temporary image enhancement, adjust brightness and contrast for legibility. The author of [#160](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/160) reported on 02.11.2026 using `grayscale(1) brightness(0.75) contrast(2.2)` on one damaged leaf. This is an example of settings for a specific image. Check against the original and save a derivative per `metric-books-scans.md` ("Faint pages").
- **Long-running in-page scripts.** Keep them in a separate tab. Navigating that tab to another address kills the task. For client-rendered pages use background tabs or a same-origin iframe.
- **Load failures.** Check the content obtained and keep a resume point per `browser-and-captcha.md`, sections 7–8.
- **No mass harvesting.** Limit requests to the task and respect limits; on `429` stop calling that source and continue other checks.
- **Form field length limits.** When filling in a form, take its actual length limits into account and check the final text: programmatic input may behave differently from manual input. Shorten or distribute explanations across suitable fields without losing material particulars; the absence of `maxlength` does not guarantee there is no server-side limit. Keep the full draft of the request in the letters folder (`letters.md`). Submitting is the user's step (`correspondence.md`); the agent never sends.
- **Scan not available by direct link.** Open it in the source's viewer. The needed evidential fragment may be saved per the source's rules, with the URL and frame number recorded on the Source.

### Checking the copy obtained

Apply these checks for a new retrieval method or on signs of an incomplete or wrong download. Files already checked and unchanged do not need a full re-audit on every search.

- **Selected frame and loaded image.** In a dynamic viewer the address and the number field may update before the image. Before saving, match the frame actually loaded to the selected document: by the available image identifier, the load-complete signal and the content. A change of URL or counter is not enough. If the correspondence is not established, keep the address as uncertain rather than assigning a new number to the old image.
- **Edges of a tiled assembly.** The absence of gaps inside the rectangle obtained does not confirm that the page edges were reached. For the needed fragment, check the outer bounds against the viewer or the available source dimensions; record the area and the resolution level. For example, an assembly with no internal holes may lack the right-hand column of tiles. A full frame need not be obtained if the task needs only a known area; do not call a partial assembly the full archival original.
- **Content format.** For a new retrieval channel, check decoding and the actual format of a sample: the extension and declared MIME type may be wrong. On a mismatch, check the affected batch and correct the description, keeping the prior version; do not convert or rename the received originals in the scratchpad to make metadata agree. The vault copy is then made per `archive.md` (`.jpg`/`.pdf`; PNG converted with `sips`). SHA-256 checks byte integrity, not the correctness of the description.
- **Substantive result of a request.** An HTTP 200 may contain a login page or an error message instead of the record. Distinguish completeness of the response, presence of the needed block, and usability of its content. If the response was cut off but the needed table was obtained in full, as confirmed by its bounds and record counter, use it with the limitations stated; do not declare the whole page obtained. Where completeness is not established, no conclusion of absence may be drawn.
- **Usability of the text layer.** Extractable text in a PDF does not guarantee correct character correspondence to the image. Before a full-text search of a new file, compare characteristic fragments with the visible text; if distorted, use a separate OCR derivative or read the image. Keep the faulty extraction as a technical version. An empty OCR result requires checking the page, not automatically declaring it blank; mass OCR of already usable text is not needed.
- **PDF geometry before OCR.** A zero page rotation (`/Rotate`) does not mean the extracted raster is oriented as in the viewer: the image placement matrix may rotate or mirror it. With a new preparation method, compare a suitable sample with the displayed page; take into account image placement, rotation and the visible area bounds. If extraction loses this geometry, use a correct page render or a transformation that respects it; for a composite page one embedded image may not be enough. Do not shrink small text to illegibility for the sake of an overview picture. Record the method, source page and OCR area in the Survey; a specific engine and division into a fixed number of crops are not required.
- **OCR coverage and reprocessing.** In batch work, distinguish catalogue links/labels, source pages, crops and the current OCR results. A repeat link to the same PDF, overlapping crops and a compiled text do not add source pages; a corrected result replaces the earlier one for the affected area rather than enlarging its coverage. Keep the page correspondence and version history (the Survey note and git). A successful run, non-empty text or a larger number of recognised characters do not establish reading accuracy; a found control word does not measure the completeness of recognition for the whole batch.
- **Correcting the preparation.** If rotation or cropping made an earlier search unusable, keep its result and the reason for revision, fix the affected derivatives and revise the negative conclusions that depended on them. Unaffected checks stand. The number of pages processed does not mean full visual reading; limits on negative results and source independence — per `evidence-protocol.md`.

## 8. When open sources are exhausted

### Event not found in the expected parish

A long series of years viewed does not by itself prove that the event happened elsewhere. Before repeating the same books or closing the branch:

- Record the actual coverage in the Survey: parish/church, years, parts of the book and frames or entries viewed; list missing years and illegible stretches separately. Use book totals where they help check coverage; a mandatory recount of all entries and a minimum number of years viewed are not needed. The general procedure for negative conclusions: `evidence-protocol.md`, section "Negative results".
- If the expected entries are missing and a survival check is useful, consider **second copies**: annual copies for the deanery, consistory or «духовное правление» (ecclesiastical board) may be described under those institutions and held in another archive or country. For Catholic metric books of Latgale, possible routes are the deaneries section of Raduraksti and the materials of НИАБ (NIAB, National Historical Archives of Belarus) (`seed-sources.md`); check the presence of the parish and period in the specific opis. A composite volume may contain extracts of many parishes for a year, and the entries of one parish may lie in volumes of different years. Determine the section you need from the table of contents, the headings and the question-relevant pages; check leaf and frame against anchor points, allowing for inserts, versos and repeat shots (section 1). Absence of the section you need leaves a coverage gap: check suitable neighbouring units and survival according to the opis. Service leaves may suggest the references of neighbouring years; check them against the catalogue. Note the copy and the coverage read; assess copies of one entry per `evidence-protocol.md`, section "Independence" (copies of one entry are one Source). Checking copies is not required for every brief search.
- With a gap for a village, consider suitable books of another church of the same village and the quires of the year in question. Check covers and group labels per `search-result-validation.md`, section "Catalogue description and movement of documents". If a combined index searches several archives, remove the custodian filter when there are grounds to look for a copy held elsewhere. If the next event could have happened in the year of the last find, check the relevant part of that same year.
- If a parish kept its entries separately by parts, check the coverage of the book by its title page, signature, households and settlements. Separate confession lists, if they exist, may suggest where the household was registered. For a marriage, choose the books of the parts relating to the groom and the bride, if there are grounds. The table of contents, the opis and available title pages help find another part or a second copy of the same year in a composite volume. Compare the composition of sections across suitable copies. Limit the conclusion to the parts actually read and state the unknown coverage of the rest.
- Choose the next route by evidence of actual residence, work, service or resettlement. A change of estate or military registration is a lead for the search, not independent proof of a move. Check neighbouring parishes and other suitable communities by the jurisdiction of the relevant period; do not assume an unchanged composition and order of books in every file.
- Allow for records moving across borders. In 1918–1920s a village's acts might go into the books of a neighbouring volost, uyezd or district, and after border changes those books end up in the archive of another region. If the village's records are not in the archive of the present oblast, find whose books its acts went into in the relevant years and check the ZAGS fonds of the neighbouring districts and regions of that period. Record for which months the village is present in the books found, and for which it is not.
  Per the observation of the author of [#150](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/150) of 02.11.2026, a volume with a general parish heading contained only some of its settlements: a note on the title page, gaps in numbering and the content of the entries indicated this. With such a contradiction, establish the actual composition per `search-result-validation.md`, section "Catalogue description and movement of documents". These signs do not by themselves prove the reason for the reduction; choose other copies and custodians by the established jurisdiction.
- Look for indirect time bounds. A remarriage entry calling a person a widow or widower may indicate the death of the earlier spouse before that entry; first establish which spouse is meant and to what date the statement refers. A note «отец умер» (father died) also requires reading the full context. A person's absence from the next household listing does not by itself prove death.
  - The formula «у крестьянина N» (to the peasant N) in a child's death entry gives a lead to the household and to a possible later mention of the father. The absence of the word «умерший» (deceased) leaves his status on the date of the act open. An explicitly named personal action or presence of an identified person may give a lower bound for death by the date of participation. For a godparent, surety or witness, compare the full formula with recording practice, possible representation, and the dates of event and registration. Use the settlement given with the name as the act's bearing for checking identity.
- Compare the family account with the documentary chronology, keeping the contradictions. A child's birth after the presumed death of the father does not by itself refute that death: distinguish birth, conception and registration date. Do not fit dates to the account and do not declare it refuted on one ambiguous indicator.
- Look for a marriage in the bride's parish too. In many confessions and places the wedding took place where the bride lived; check the custom for the confession and period rather than treating it as a rule. If the groom's parish has no entry, consider the parish of the bride and her kin, as well as separate series of premarital investigations (section 1) and the second copies described above.
- If a baptism is not found, use the parents' marriage, the births of siblings and later age statements as bearings for a search range, keeping the grounds and the uncertainty. The parents' marriage does not by itself exclude an earlier birth, and the births of other children do not set strict bounds. An age in later documents may be understated as well as overstated. Note separately the actual coverage of books, years missing online and illegible stretches; do not turn the absence of a find in the entries viewed into an exact year of birth. Record the result as a hypothesis with a range and its grounds (Hypothesis note; on the Person, `born:` as a range or `c. YYYY` only once justified).

Choose useful checks according to the specific gap rather than going through every route mechanically. If the record is absent from the volume viewed, say exactly that; it is not the same as asserting "did not die in this parish".

Describe what is established, what is unknown, the coverage and the limitations. Put the open questions for the family and ready search links for blocked routes into the Search Plan, and draft archive requests in the letters folder (never sent by the agent).

In the report to the user, limit the conclusion to the checks performed: if the useful available checks are complete, only that volume is exhausted. List routes waiting on the user separately per `browser-and-captcha.md`, sections 5–5a; do not call them exhausted.

Close the cycle only after checking the useful available alternatives within the task. Keep the remaining queue in the `Search Plan -` note so work can resume from it (`SKILL.md` § Errors, "Routes exhausted"). An unanswered question or the unavailability of one source does not stop the other checks; repeating searches already done without new grounds is not needed.

### Coverage of resettlement-office files along the settlers' route

A file on issuing or repaying loans («ссуды») describes a particular operation. First establish from the headings, forms and content whom it recorded. The absence of a family from such a file limits the conclusion to that operation and the volume read. To trace the journey, consider documents on movement or registration, if such series survive. If correspondence indicates that a list was sent to the institution at the place of settlement, check the corresponding incoming documents at the recipient.

With an unclear heading of a thematic file, journals of incoming papers and the internal inventory may help. Check which papers the journal records for the relevant period and where their number is given. If the inventory number matches an earlier numbering inside the file, use it to go to the leaf. Sampled frames show the places read. Describing coverage and checking a derivative index — per `search-result-validation.md` and `evidence-protocol.md`, section "Negative results".

## 9. Soviet civil biography: work, the home front and cities

For a twentieth-century person, choose the route by the known dates, place of residence, employer and question. Military databases, metric books and civil acts remain useful within their coverage; the civil biography complements them:

- Regional and national archives: award lists, records of evacuees, fonds of enterprises, institutions and educational establishments. An award index may give the place of work but is not a census of all employees. Distinguish nomination, award and presentation, the date of each event and the source of the line.
- **Soviet maternal awards from 1944.** The title «Мать-героиня» (Mother Heroine) — ten or more children; the Order «Материнская слава» (Maternal Glory) III, II and I class — seven, eight and nine; the «Медаль материнства» (Motherhood Medal) II and I class — five and six. Establishment and statutes — [GA RF documents of 8 July and 18 August 1944](https://statearchive.ru/tests/06.html); the original text of the statutes — [«Ведомости Верховного Совета СССР», 1944, № 45](https://sun.tsu.ru/mminfo/2020/000462771/1944/1944_045.pdf). Lists in the local press and handbooks give a lead by the mother's surname, which may differ from the children's surnames. Look for questionnaires, nominations and information on the children in the opisi of the presidiums of the Supreme Soviets and local executive committees; for example, the [guide to the fond of the Tomsk oblast executive committee](https://guides.rusarchives.ru/funds/127/tomskiy-oblastnoy-sovet-narodnyh-deputatov-i-ego-ispolnitelnyy-komitet-oblsovet) describes questionnaires and registration cards of mothers of many children. Check the presence of a list of children, its fields, custodian and access for the specific document. An award does not establish the full composition of the family or biological kinship: take into account the version of the statute on the event date, adoption, and the exceptions provided for deceased or missing children. Do not derive the youngest child's date of birth from the date of a published list alone: first establish what is dated, which children are counted and what conditions applied; the one-year-old rule by itself does not give an exact date of birth.
- Factory histories and biographical dictionaries, factory newspapers, the local press, museums of enterprises and educational establishments. If only a quotation is available, find its original; the quotation and the source book do not become independent confirmations. Absence of an article in a selective reference work does not refute employment at the enterprise.
- The employer's fond: orders, staff and personnel files, if they survive and are accessible. For a draft request use the known full name, years and place of work; do not invent what is unknown. Check access to personnel files under the institution's rules.
- **Party registration.** If a party registration card exists, check what particulars of work, service and posts its form contains. Distinguish the dates of events from when the card was compiled. Check later particulars against available documents on work and service, keeping discrepancies. A unit's route helps check the chronology. To search for party documents, use the РГАСПИ (RGASPI, Russian State Archive of Socio-Political History) fonds catalogue listed in `seed-sources.md` and the opisi of local party registration by place and period.
- Family city memorials can give a photo and a biographical lead. Evaluate the text and the attached documents separately per `evidence-protocol.md`, and conduct the photo search per `photo-search.md`.

The Chelyabinsk collections in `seed-sources.md`, section "Soviet period: labour, home front, cities", are a regional example. For Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan and other former republics, look for fonds by the place of the event and the successor archive, including the places of departure and arrival in an evacuation or resettlement. Use local names and languages per `ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, rather than limiting yourself to Russian databases.

## 10. The child's surname or patronymic does not match the named father

A discrepancy between the family account and the child's documents does not by itself allow choosing a version or declaring the account mistaken. Keep separate hypotheses in the Hypothesis note, distinguishing what is directly recorded from the researcher's explanation:

- information on the father is absent or was entered under the registration rules in force at the time;
- the documents reflect a stepfather, an adoptive father or a later change of name;
- the name is rendered in another linguistic form; the correspondence is checked against documents and local context (`ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, sections 3–4);
- the account or an index mixes up different people, or the record was misread.

In the USSR, the [decree of 8 July 1944, arts. 19–21](https://sun.tsu.ru/mminfo/2020/000462771/1944/1944_037.pdf) tied the rights and duties of spouses to registration of the marriage and abolished the mother's right to sue to establish paternity and for maintenance if she had not been in a registered marriage with the presumed father. For the child of a mother without a registered marriage, the decree provided for her surname and a patronymic as she indicated. It also provided for registering earlier de facto marital relations, stating the length of cohabitation. These norms help read the original act and later annotations. Neither the biological father nor the fact of cohabitation can be determined from the absence of an entry for the father or from the patronymic.

The [law of 27 June 1968, arts. 1–3](https://dspace.nplg.gov.ge/bitstream/1234/512792/1/Zaria_Vostoka_1968_N150.pdf) brought the «Основы» (Fundamentals of marriage and family legislation) into force from 1 October 1968. The court procedure under art. 16 of the Fundamentals applied to children born after their introduction. For earlier births it provided for a joint application by the mother and the person acknowledging paternity, and on that person's death, judicial establishment of the fact of acknowledgement under the conditions set by law. Compare the date of birth, the date and grounds of the change to the record, and the norms of the relevant republic and period. A later registration of marriage does not by itself explain the earlier entry.

Choose distinguishing records by place and period; these are routes, not a mandatory sequence:

1. The available record of the child's birth and its annotations of changes. They can explain who is named and in what capacity; the limits of inference from an annotation — `evidence-protocol.md`.
2. Suitable records of the mother's marriage, change of name or adoption, if such a version is well founded. Clarify the custodian and access conditions for the specific jurisdiction and period; a closed record does not stop other checks.
3. Documents of the presumed father under well-founded name variants: historical house registers and directories (`source-strategy.md`, section 5), information on service, resettlement or repression, where this follows from the question and the material at hand.
4. Brothers, sisters or other relatives mentioned in the material as a distinguishing cluster; a shared surname, patronymic or address does not by itself establish kinship.

Absence of a record in a particular database does not choose a version. Distinguish documentary, legal and biological kinship per the general protocol; limit sensitive conclusions and information on living people by the rules of `privacy-and-safety.md`.
