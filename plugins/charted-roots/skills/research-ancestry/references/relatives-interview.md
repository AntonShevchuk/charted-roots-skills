# Talking with relatives about family history

Use these pointers when the user is preparing a conversation with a relative, or when family memory could clarify a specific question. Choose a small set of topics by the goal and by the answers already obtained. Preparing the conversation can run in parallel with the documentary search that is available.

Before planning, check what the vault says about whether the relative is alive (Person note, Research Notes, the user's statements); if unknown, keep it unknown and plan conditionally (`SKILL.md` § Workflow, step 1). The relative is usually a living person: `privacy-and-safety.md`, section 2, applies throughout.

Start with a free account of the episode in question. Then ask only about material gaps. For example:

- "How did you yourself learn about this? What do you remember personally, and what did you hear from others?" This helps establish where the information comes from.
- "How did the family tell these two people apart? In what situation did you meet each of them?" The answer may give features for separating namesakes.
- "What event do you connect this move with? What came before and after it?" Such a sequence helps narrow the period while keeping the dates imprecise.
- "Is there a letter, an inscription on a photograph or another family record of this episode?" If the material has already been provided, refer to it by its existing locator (Source, scan in the archive or media folder).

When the task includes a family narrative for a reader, one can ask for a remembered scene, a household habit or the person's words. Choose details the interviewee is willing to share and that bear on the task. Discuss the ready research version only after the open answer, keeping track of the prompt's influence.

**Written questions.** For an open answer, keep the presumed name, date or place in the `Search Plan -` note together with the feature that will distinguish the versions — not in the question. In the message use a relationship the recipient understands, for example "your father", and add a known neutral reference point if needed. If a prompted follow-up is useful, prepare it separately, after the open answer. Keep the context of the question and assess the provenance of the answer by `field-lessons.md`, section 6, and `evidence-protocol.md`. Give the draft to the user; the agent never sends it.

Before a new message, check the answers this person has already given (vault Sources of `source_type: oral_history`, the Search Plan). Another relative may be asked the same thing if their testimony helps distinguish the versions. Keep the previous answer in the vault and the new question open. Respect a refusal to answer on a topic. If the questions will be read out over the phone, put the most important first.

**Recording the answers.** An interview or written answer is a record: bring it in via `add-archive-record` as a Source with `source_type: oral_history` — transcription (or the written answer) in the archive folder, the Source body stating the date of the conversation, who told it, from whom they heard it, and the context of each material answer; then one Citation per person × fact it bears on. In the transcription distinguish personal memory, retelling and supposition. Keep unknown dates and relationships unknown. Treat oral testimony as a record with provenance, never as documentary fact (`SKILL.md` § Evidence rules, rule 7); compare it with documents in the Hypothesis note. A comparison with a document may confirm part of the account and leave other details open.

**Living people in the record.** Minimum data on the interviewee and anyone living they mention: name and relationship as needed for the question; no current addresses, phones, accounts, ID numbers or data on minors. Sensitive disclosures (adoption, paternity, repression, illness) — neutral wording and only what the question needs (`privacy-and-safety.md`, sections 2, 3, 5).

Open-question techniques and the assessment of prompts are described in `field-lessons.md`, section 6. Provenance, dependence of testimony and claim statuses are determined by `evidence-protocol.md`. Data on living people, sensitive discoveries and external correspondence are governed by `privacy-and-safety.md`.
