# Validating search results and archival references

Read this when reusing a saved search, when a catalogue and a book contradict each other, for finds made through indexes, or on a suspected error in an archival reference. For a direct link already verified, the ordinary Source is enough; repeating the whole search without new grounds is not needed.

## What question the sample answers

- **Applicability of an earlier query.** Before a negative conclusion, check the original filters, the roles of the people, the years, the territory, the limit and the results actually viewed (as recorded in the Survey). A sample of marriages by the groom's name is not a list of all marriages in the parish and does not close a search for another groom. A positive find in it can be investigated in a targeted way; a negative conclusion needs suitable coverage. This does not require trying every combination of names and sources. After correcting a name, role, date or place, review the applicability of the checks that depended on that feature and choose useful targeted repeats, keeping the original queries and results; record the new search in the Survey beside the earlier one, with the grounds for the correction. Acts already read and independent checks stand, but an identification of a person based on the corrected feature needs reassessing (in the Hypothesis).
- **Meaning of an indexed field.** A field name does not replace reading the form. If a children's book gives an age next to the year of registration, the result of a "year of birth" filter cannot automatically be taken as a complete list of those born that year. Compare specific lines and headings; do not declare the whole index wrong on one example and do not calculate an exact date of birth from a rounded age.
- **Unverified loading of results.** A table with headings and no rows in an HTML tool may mean the results did not load, including when loaded by script; by itself it establishes neither the cause nor the absence of records. The author of [#62](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/62) reported such an observation in Geneteka on 28.10.2026 without re-checking in a browser. With unknown completeness, record the result in the Survey as *partial* per `evidence-protocol.md`, and as *blocked* when an obstacle is established. If a browser is available (and web work is authorised), check the loading and the counter; otherwise keep this route in the Search Plan and continue other checks.
- **Control query.** If an empty result was obtained by a new loading method, with a different encoding or a new parameter, test it on a record known to be present in the same accessible index. A common name by itself does not guarantee a positive control. An empty control does not establish the cause of failure and does not prove the person's absence: record the result and the limitation of the check, as *partial* or *blocked* according to the actual state. A successful control confirms that this query works, but not the completeness of coverage for the family sought.
- **Results that do not match the query.** Per the observation of the author of [#158](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/158) of 03.11.2026, after a series of queries Geneteka returned, for an exact surname, many records with other surnames. The author of [#160](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/160) reported a similar mismatch with the territorial filter and the surname on 02.11.2026, and normal results from the standard form on 03.11.2026. The Arolsen API without a session cookie, per the observation of the author of #158, returned zero or the count of the whole fond. The cause of these responses has not been independently established: they do not prove protective substitution, a safe number of queries or the absence of the person sought. Compare the query conditions, the counter and the actual rows; record a mismatched result neither as a find nor as a negative result. Keep the query and the limitation in the Survey as *partial* / *blocked* per the existing order, then use the available standard form or another source; on signs of a block stop querying per `browser-and-captcha.md`, section 7. Do not repeat queries in series to establish a threshold.
- **Church dedication and place name.** An opis heading such as «книга Дмитриевской церкви» (book of the St Demetrius church) may name the dedication (a church in the name of a saint) in another village, not a settlement with a similar name. Establish the parish and the village from the full heading of the file, the opis and the entries of the book; until then, do not assign entries found in it to the like-named hamlet and do not use its empty result as a negative result for that hamlet.
- **Unit of matching.** Clarify whether the service searches by record, page or whole document. Two search words on one page of an opis may relate to different files. Check that they occur together within the relevant entry. Match the page given by an index to the available PDF version by characteristic text and file number; a discrepancy in numbers does not by itself explain its cause.

## Rootera: masked fields and placeholders

The author of [#76](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/76) reported an observation of masked fields on Rootera on 28.10.2026 and passed on an explanation from support dated 29.10.2026. According to that explanation, before a plan is activated the server does not transmit the record's geography and estate: the interface shows, blurred, the placeholder values «Малта» (Maltas) and «Крестьянин» (peasant). Activation reveals the actual information; it does not change the record's geography. This is a dated report by the author, not an independent check of the server implementation; do not carry it over automatically to other services or later versions of the interface.

- Do not record these placeholders as a person's residence, origin or estate, and do not choose an archival route by them. A masked field stays unknown; even legible text extracted from the DOM does not make it record data.
- With an active plan available (and the user's authorisation), read the open fields and check them against the archival file and frame given. Without access, continue with other sources, keeping the limitation of this check in the Survey.
- If a placeholder has already entered the vault (a Survey, Hypothesis, Source or Person note), keep the history of the observation (git), correct its interpretation and the related search conclusions. The appearance of a different value after activation is not by itself an error of geography in the database or evidence of the family moving.

## Catalogue description and movement of documents

When choosing between several files or assessing gaps, compare the relevant place, confession, period and act type in the existing Search Plan or Survey, for example in a compact table. Keep the reference and the source of the description; distinguish the years and sections claimed by the catalogue, the availability of a scan, and the years, sections and leaves/frames actually viewed, with illegible or skipped stretches. Leave the unknown unknown: an available scan does not mean a full viewing, and the absence of a file or year in the catalogue checked does not establish loss or the absence of other copies. Limits of a negative conclusion — per `evidence-protocol.md`; keep the next useful check in the Search Plan, without a separate note. Standing finding aids and fond/opis lists that are worth consulting again go into a `Reference -` note (`research.md`).

- **Conflict between levels of description.** The general catalogue heading, a nested volume, a digital group and a section of a book can differ in place, dates or act type. On a specific contradiction, compare the nested entry, the title or service frame and a substantive opening of the relevant section. Where the catalogue date may be wrong, check the cover or title of a suitable neighbouring file and the notes marking the start and end of the book. The sequence of references, a discrepancy in ages and a volunteer index help choose what to open. Verify the dating against the title and the content. Keep both descriptions and the grounds for the choice. Choose suitable volumes taking the nested descriptions and the question into account. A database's name may reflect today's region while its actual coverage is a historical guberniya or diocese. Variants of parish series and copies — in `field-lessons.md`, section 8.
- **Suspected typo in a reference.** Keep the original reference, the contradictions and the possible replacement separately. Check the place, date span, document type and size of the file. A reference to leaf 61 in a file of eight leaves requires checking the numbering and survival: the size by itself does not establish the last leaf number. A similar number with a fitting place and year remains a candidate when the date of the act falls outside its range. Confirm a replacement by content or an established correspondence of references, not by similarity of number. Never rewrite the `page:` of an existing Citation or the Source title to the corrected reference (`CLAUDE.md`); record the correction in the Source body.
- **Removal and attachment.** A note «подложено к другому делу, в его составе выбыло в другой фонд» (attached to another file, removed with it to another fond) sets out a chain of movements. Keep separately the old number, the number in the opis, the intermediate file and the destination fond. Until the final number is established, say so and use the chain for further searching or for a draft request for the correspondence (in the letters folder). A transfer does not prove the document's loss and does not authorise automatically choosing a like-named file in the new fond.

## A find through a bibliographic index

Separate the particulars of the work from those of the index describing it: the year of an article may differ from the year of the index, the printed page from the PDF page. Do not carry an editor's clarification in brackets into the original title without checking the full text. Before ordering a copy, check against the image the material numerical particulars if they were obtained by OCR or are in doubt. Obtaining the index does not mean obtaining the article itself; after obtaining the full text, note separately the new information and the limits of identifying the author.
