# Reading metric-book scans

Choose the mode by the request. To transcribe a single page or entry, an answer with the locator and readability caveats is enough. When searching a book, report the finds and the actual coverage. Use vault material already in place (Sources, transcriptions, the `Search Plan -` and `Survey -` notes) where the task needs it. Before reading, check the research folder for a `Reference -` note on the register's printed form (column layout of the book type and period).

## Reading a page or an entry

- From the overall view of the page, establish the columns, the numbers and the boundaries of the entry needed. If the act continues on the other side of the opening or on a neighbouring frame, match the headings and the number. For small text, open a zoom with enough context to tie the name and role to the line. If the user asks for the whole page to be read, checking the list of acts against the image helps catch a missed entry.
- When a hyphenated break is suspected, match the end of the line against the start of the next, even if there is no hyphen. For a disputed letter, especially a capital, compare the visible strokes with clear examples of the same hand for the plausible readings. If there are few samples, keep the variants. The family's earlier reading may influence new transcriptions. Read each material entry by its own strokes and note where a known variant influenced the reading. Distinguish first name, patronymic and surname from the full entry and its columns. Show the original spelling, the normalisation and any restoration from another document separately.
- Render the visible part of a word and mark where the reading is doubtful, the text illegible or the field absent. Test a variant proposed by the user against the visible strokes and keep the uncertainty if they are insufficient. The procedure for re-checking a disputed reading is in `field-lessons.md`, section 1, "Checking a visual reading". In the vault, doubtful text goes into the transcription's `## Uncertain readings` (`archive.md`).
- Give the locator provided or visible: file, PDF page, folio or frame, act number. For the date, take into account the separate fields for birth, baptism, death and burial, and where needed check the section heading against the image. For a brief request the fields needed are enough; for a verbatim extract keep the structure and the original orthography as far as the scan allows.

## Searching a book

From the title page, anchor frames and the structure of the book, establish the relevant sections, years and columns. Choose them by the question. Within the chosen scope keep the material candidates, including namesakes still to be told apart (in the Hypothesis note, not as Person notes). A search strip or OCR helps choose a frame to check. Check an important find against the full image of the entry with the context needed. Preserving images and working with overview strips are described in `field-lessons.md`, section 1 ("Selective preservation of evidence", "Single-column overview strips"; strips via `scripts/scan_montage.py`).

When handing over the result, distinguish a quick screening, a detailed reading and gaps. State the sections, frames and columns actually viewed, and the illegible places. For a long search, keep the next stretch in the existing `Search Plan -` note and the coverage in the `Survey -` note. The procedure for recording coverage and negative results is in `research-coordination.md`, section "Extended scan reading", and `evidence-protocol.md`, section "Negative results". A record found goes into the vault via `add-archive-record`.

## Loading frames and indexes

Check that the viewer's content is ready, and what to do on failure, by `browser-and-captcha.md`, section 2, "Loading frames and indexes". Include in the coverage the fragments actually viewed, keeping the gaps as above. If the user commissioned a search of the index only, keep to that scope and count cards that did not load as unchecked.

## Faint pages

Keep the original file separate. If ordinary zoom is not enough, try a gentle contrast change or viewing a single colour channel on a copy of the fragment needed. Compare the processed version with the original: excessive sharpening, binarisation and generative processing can remove fine strokes or add false detail. If there are still few visible traces, mark the gap or choose an available copy of better quality. Choose the tool and the amount of processing by readability and the task.
