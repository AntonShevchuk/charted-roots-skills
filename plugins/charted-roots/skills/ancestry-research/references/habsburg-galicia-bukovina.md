# Galicia and Bukovina under the Habsburgs and after

Read this file when a family is connected with Galicia (including the Hutsul, Boyko and Lemko mountain districts) or Bukovina: Austrian metric books, Habsburg conscription lists, land cadastres, the transition between Austrian, Polish, Romanian and Soviet rule.

## 1. Jurisdictional chain

1. Galicia (the Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria) — Habsburg from 1772. Bukovina — under Habsburg rule from 1775, later part of Galicia, from 1849 a separate duchy.
2. Internal division: Kronland → Kreis/Politischer Bezirk («повят», powiat) → gmina (commune) → parish.
3. After the collapse of Austria-Hungary, allow for the transitional authorities and wars of 1918–1923; in the interwar period Eastern Galicia was Poland (voivodeships Lwów, Stanisławów, Tarnopol), Bukovina was Romania.
4. Eastern Galicia came under Soviet rule in 1939, Northern Bukovina in 1940; during the war of 1941–1944 different occupation administrations operated. After the war Eastern Galicia and Northern Bukovina were the Ukrainian SSR; the western part of historical Galicia was Poland, Southern Bukovina Romania. Do not carry one chain over to the whole region.
5. For each event record the jurisdiction on the event date: the language of the form, the calendar, the registering institution and the successor archive all depend on it. In the vault, the identified chain goes into Place notes per `CLAUDE.md` (Kronland → `state`, Bezirk/powiat → `county`, gmina/village → `locality`; former names in `aliases:`); an anachronistic chain is a schema decision, not a fix.

## 2. Metric books

1. In the Austrian period, rules for keeping metric books were introduced from 1784; check their application to a confession, and the existence of earlier books, by the specific parish and inventory.
2. Confessions: Greek Catholic (most rural families of the mountain districts), Roman Catholic (Poles, Germans), Orthodox (widespread in Bukovina, isolated in Galicia), Jewish, Lutheran (German colonists).
3. Forms in Latin and Polish; names in Polish/Latin transcription: *Wasyl*, *Hryc*, *Iwan*, *Andrej*, the ending *-czuk* instead of «-чук». Build the variant matrix by `ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, sections 3–4.
4. Consistory copies (duplicates submitted to the consistory) have often survived where the parish book is lost; in archive catalogues they are separate units — check both. A parish entry and its consistory duplicate are copies of one record and never corroborate each other (`SKILL.md` § Evidence rules, 1).
5. The transition to civil registration came in the interwar period (Poland, Romania) and the Soviet period; check exact dates and the dates books were transferred to archives by place and on the archive's site.

## 3. Calendar — the region's main trap

Do not infer the calendar of a metric entry from the territory's state affiliation alone. The civil calendar, the liturgical calendar and the practice of a specific book could differ by confession, parish and period.

- Check the title, any indication of Old/New Style, double dates, neighbouring entries and the collection description. The liturgical calendar alone does not prove how the scribe dated the book.
- A 13-day difference is a hypothesis of calendar conversion for the relevant period, not proof that two dates or two people are the same. In the 19th century the difference could be 12 days; check the specific year and the month boundary.
- Record both dates as written in the transcription, and state in the Source body that the calendar is unknown if it has not been established. Put the ISO value on the Event only once checked, noting the calendar in the Source body; if unclear, leave the contradiction open in the Hypothesis (`ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, section 5).
- Matching a birth, a baptism and a date from a later document also requires checking the event type: baptism may have been later than birth. The technique in `field-lessons.md`, section 2, does not replace this check.

## 4. Military sources

1. **Casualty lists 1914–1919** (Verlustlisten): route and limitations — `field-lessons.md`, section 4, and `seed-sources.md`, "Military sources". Use them where the person has an actual connection with military service; absence from the lists does not prove absence of service.
2. **Conscription books and service records** (Musterungslisten, Grundbuchblätter, Stammrollen): custody depends on period, unit, rank and place of enrolment. After the 1868 reform registration was decentralised; many documents of men born 1865–1900 remained in the successor states, and a large part of the Galician service records is lost. Check the Kriegsarchiv, ЦДІАЛ (TsDIAL, Central State Historical Archive of Ukraine in Lviv — including fond 780) and other suitable archives; establish whether a digital copy exists on FamilySearch by the specific collection. Basis — the official Kriegsarchiv handbook, sections 2.2.1.2, 2.2.1.11, 5.3.2 (see below).
3. **Captivity in Russia 1914–1918.** Questionnaires and lists of Central Powers prisoners of war in Russian and Soviet archives (РГВИА / RGVIA, ГАРФ / GARF, regional fonds) often give the family composition and home village as stated by the prisoner — sometimes the only such document.
4. Place of birth alone does not determine the army served in: allow for relocation, citizenship, voluntary service and captivity. Confirm army affiliation by a document.

## 5. Land cadastres (fassions)

For rural families, the best companion to the metric books:

1. **The Josephine fassion** (1785–1789): a cadastral census of Galicia, households by name.
2. **The Franciscan metrica** (1819–1820; subsequent cadastral surveys and updates are separate series): owners, plots, their value.
3. Cadastral maps (Feldskizzen) and per-plot specifications.

The fassions show the house and its number, the owners' names, the size of the holding and the division of a homestead among heirs. This helps separate namesakes and find brothers who did not enter the metric-book line of search. Custody — ЦДІАЛ and district archives; some scans on FamilySearch.

## 6. Toponyms and gazetteers

1. The Austrian gazetteers of Galicia (19th-century Orts-Repertorium) and the interwar «Skorowidz miejscowości Rzeczypospolitej Polskiej» (1934) — for finding the official spelling and the assignment to powiat and parish.
2. Mountain homesteads often had their own names and were not separate communes — look for them by parish and cadastre, not in a modern gazetteer. Until such a homestead is identified, it stays unidentified in the Hypothesis or Survey; do not invent a parent chain for it.
3. Mapire (see `seed-sources.md`) — Habsburg military surveys and cadastral maps overlaid on a modern map: visual matching of old and new toponyms.
4. Names changed with the authority: Austrian → interwar Polish → Soviet; one village may appear in documents in three forms. Record the form as in the source (`SKILL.md`, Workflow step 2; Evidence rules, 4) and keep the other forms in the Place note's `aliases:`.

## 7. Where things are held

| What to look for | Where |
|---|---|
| Austrian metric books of Galicia (Greek Catholic, Roman Catholic, Jewish) | ЦДІАЛ (Lviv), catalogues of metric books on its site; some on FamilySearch; some books stayed in parishes or went to Poland |
| Metric books of Northern Bukovina | State Archive of Chernivtsi Oblast; specific books — by inventories and the FamilySearch catalogue |
| Metric books of Southern Bukovina | Romanian archives and parishes at the place of the event; check jurisdiction and survival separately |
| Interwar metric books (Polish, Romanian) | The same regional archives; later civil books — with ZAGS / ДРАЦС (DRATsS, the Ukrainian civil registry) |
| Galicia before 1772 (Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth) | AGAD (Warsaw), ЦДІАЛ and other archives according to the creating institution; Bukovina had a different jurisdictional history |
| Fassions and cadastres | ЦДІАЛ and district archives |
| Habsburg conscription books and service records | Kriegsarchiv and archives of the successor states; check survival and fond, see section 4 |
| Roman Catholic books (some) | ЦДІАЛ, AGAD, regional archives and parishes; Matricula — only if the relevant parish is in its catalogue |
| Deportations of 1939–1941 and repression | Regional publications of the SBU sectoral archives (for example, «Нескорена Буковина» — see `seed-sources.md`); method — `field-lessons.md`, section 3 |

## 8. Reading entries

Kinship formulae, language variants and scan-reading techniques are in `field-lessons.md`, sections 1–2, and `ex-ussr-locale-guide.md`, sections 3–4. Do not duplicate them here. When the language or form changes, identify the columns afresh and note the confession and calendar of the specific book.

## 9. Typical route for a mountain family

1. From the village, period and confession, determine the parish and archive; for a military question, additionally search the Verlustlisten to refine the Heimatort.
2. By village in the ЦДІАЛ catalogues — which metric books and years survive and whether they are on FamilySearch.
3. Read the births of the relevant surname across the whole range (`field-lessons.md`, section 1), separating namesakes by house and godparents. Record the sweep as a `Survey -` note.
4. In parallel, the fassions and cadastres: households and plots; for the Franciscan metrica — 1819–1820, look for later surveys separately.
5. Military service records — if the person served; choose the archive by the rules of section 4.
6. For the interwar generation — Polish/Romanian metric books and the deportation lists of 1939–1941.

## 10. Basis and limits of verification

- [Official Kriegsarchiv handbook on genealogy](https://www.statearchives.gv.at/dam/jcr:42e3b4ef-a2ed-4085-ae66-b3e0de666b75/Genealogie%20im%20Kriegsarchiv.pdf), 2006, sections 2.2.1.2, 2.2.1.11 and 5.3.2: distribution of military personnel records and losses. This is a historical handbook; check current addresses and access afresh.
- [ЦДІАЛ, fond inventories](https://tsdial.archives.gov.ua/index5.html): check fond and years; distinguish the Josephine and Franciscan metricas from later cadastral series.
- [UGCC on the 2023 calendar reform](https://pdf.ugcc.ua/data/kalendar-sogodni-rozpochynaetsya-nova-storinka-v-istoriy-ugkts-2286/): an example of the difference between church calendar tradition and the state calendar. It does not determine the calendar of an individual archival record.
- [State Archive of Chernivtsi Oblast, documents on the change of power in 1940](https://cv.archives.gov.ua/vozednannyaBukovini.html): confirm dates from the documents; separate Soviet propaganda rhetoric in the quoted materials from the fact of the change of jurisdiction.
- [Matricula, current portal contents](https://data.matricula-online.eu/en/): check country, diocese and parish; the presence of the relevant collection is not assumed.
