# Searching for photographs of a person

Read this file during the search step (`SKILL.md` § Workflow, step 4). Photo search runs in the main pass alongside the documents: it is an item on the coverage checklist, not a separate task after the report.

## 1. Where to look

Start with the family's material; assess the reliability of each picture and caption separately (`evidence-protocol.md`).

1. **The user's documents and albums.** Photos appear in passports, ID cards, passes, membership, trade-union and labour books (трудовые книжки), student cards, military cards (военные билеты). Examine the pictures and the inscriptions on the backs in material already provided; crop copies while keeping the originals intact. Note missing backs and carry on searching.
2. **Pages and trees kept by the family.** FamilySearch, Geni, MyHeritage; memorial databases by type of case: «Бессмертный полк» ("Immortal Regiment") and «Дорога памяти» ("Road of Memory") for war participants, «Бессмертный барак» ("Immortal Barracks") and «Последний адрес» ("Last Address") for the repressed, Yad Vashem Pages of Testimony for Holocaust victims. Use the sites' own search (`source-strategy.md`, section 12).
2a. **Registration cards in «Память народа» ("Memory of the People").** The author of [#91](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/91) reported on 30.10.2026 that records from the service-registration card index (учётно-послужная картотека) and military commissariats sometimes carried photographs even when the card scan itself was closed. Check the available image and its link to the specific record; keep the URL and the record type. The note «Документ находится в: ЦАМО» ("Document held in: TsAMO") is a route to the holder, with no guarantee that a better image exists or will be released; request terms — `correspondence.md`.
3. **Group photographs.** Websites and museums of schools, technical colleges, enterprises, collective farms, church and Jewish communities; graduation and workplace group photos; anniversary volumes; Yizkor books of shtetls.
4. **Gravestones.** Twentieth-century monuments often carry a photo medallion. Cemetery databases and cemetery-photography projects. In photos from funeral agencies' open reports on relatives' funerals, neighbouring monuments get into the frame (`field-lessons.md`, section 5).
5. **Local press and obituaries.** District newspapers, factory newsletters (многотиражки), archive websites with photo collections.
6. **Files that may contain photographs.** Passport files, exit and naturalisation applications, employee personnel files, student files. Whether a photo is present depends on the country, period, file type and survival. A repressed person's investigation file may contain front and profile shots, but this is not guaranteed. If relevant to the task, check the available inventories and scans; report finds tactfully. For a file that is not accessible, draft an archive request (`correspondence.md`).
7. **Archival photo collections.** Check catalogues and digital collections; for a picture with no accessible copy, draft a request citing the reference found.
8. **Auctions and flea markets.** When it helps the question, search public lots of historical photographs, postcards and documents by surname, place and photo studio. Check the inscription on the item itself and the seller's description separately. For a material find, keep the URL, the lot number, the date viewed and a fragment permitted by the platform's rules (Survey note; a Source if it becomes evidence). The description and a watermark help trace the original publication of a forwarded picture. Correspondence, bids and purchase only within a separate instruction from the user — the agent drafts messages, never sends or bids. Example platforms — in `seed-sources.md`.

## 2. Identification

- Base an identification on the photograph's **caption and provenance**: the page author's caption, the inscription on the back, the caption in a book or archival inventory. Check them against the documents; a caption by itself can be wrong.
- **Do not apply face recognition** or reverse image search to photographs of living people. A "looks like / doesn't look like" comparison is not evidence.
- Record an **identification by a relative** ("that's him") as oral testimony: who identified the person, when, and on what basis — personal acquaintance, a caption, or comparison with family photos. Assess provenance and dependence per `evidence-protocol.md`, section 6; a separate story or teller does not automatically create an independent chain. Resemblance by itself establishes neither identity nor kinship; assess the link between picture and record and between person and family on the available evidence, keeping the uncertainty.
- On a group photo, record who is named in the caption and where the caption comes from ("per the family's caption — second from the left in the front row").
- If the caption conflicts with the documents (place, year), keep both versions and mark the discrepancy. Example: the back says «Киев, 1930» ("Kyiv, 1930"), but according to the documents the family moved to Kyiv in 1934.

## 3. Dating

Record a dating as a hypothesis with its grounds.

### General indicators

- **Photo studio.** A studio stamp or embossing on the mount and the back: the city and owner's name can be checked against directories and address calendars (адрес-календари) that give the studio's years of operation.
- **Format and finish.** Compare the base material, mount and edges with dated examples from the relevant country; a single feature does not fix a hard date.
- **Clothing, hairstyles, interiors** — only as a rough guide, with caution.
- **Photo from a document.** If it is confirmed that the picture was pasted in at issue, it was taken no later than that date; the issue date is not the date the photo was taken. Check separately for a later replacement photo or a copy.
- **File date from a messenger.** A date in a file name or message does not by itself establish when the photo was taken: it may refer to the message, the forwarding, the export or the creation of the digital copy. Per the observation of the author of [#193](https://github.com/DenisSergeevitch/ex-ussr-kinship-research/pull/193) of 08.11.2026, in his Telegram export old family photos and letters carried forwarding dates from the 2020s. Establish the meaning of the date from the available export fields and the file's provenance; if it is unknown, say so. Keep an established date of the message or copy separate from the dating of the picture by its caption, content and provenance. Keep the original file, its name and the relevant fragment of the message, without unrelated correspondence.

### Military uniform (if the person served)

- **Shoulder boards of the 1943 pattern** (погоны образца 1943 года) were introduced in the Red Army (RKKA) in January 1943 ([history of the reform, Military Historical Museum of Artillery](https://museum-artillery.ru/ru/temporal/exhibitions-archive/pogonyi.html)). The lower bound "not before 1943" applies only to a confirmed picture of an RKKA serviceman in the uniform of his own time; it does not apply to Imperial or foreign uniform, costumes or re-enactments.
- **Collar tabs with "kubari" and "shpaly"** (петлицы с «кубарями» и «шпалами» — squares and bars) are typical of RKKA uniform before the 1943 reform; the switch to the new insignia was not instantaneous, so do not fix an exact date by this feature alone.
- The rank on the shoulder boards (number of stars and stripes) can be checked against the dates of promotion in the documents.
- Russian Imperial Army uniform: arm of service and rank from shoulder boards and cuffs — per uniform reference works.

## 4. Download and storage

- Keep relevant historical photos and fragments within the research, if the source permits. No separate permission per file is needed once web work for the task is authorised (`privacy-and-safety.md`, section 0).
- Take the highest resolution available through the viewer or the link to the original; artificial upscaling adds no detail.
- Save the photo in the media folder as `.jpg` or `.pdf` only — convert PNG with `sips` (JPEG, quality 95) — under a clear, unique filename describing the picture or period (`archive.md` § Scan files). A photo that came with a document (enclosed in a file, scanned from an ID) belongs with that document's scan in the archive folder, via `add-archive-record`.
- Describe the photo in the vault, not in a README: on the **Source** (`source_type: photo`) — what is shown, the dating and its grounds, provenance (URL or document), size, notes on the caption and discrepancies. State original vs copy (primary vs derivative) in the Source body by the established provenance (`evidence-protocol.md`) — do not automatically treat any file as an image of the original; `confidence` rates the reading. Link it from the **Person**'s media (`charted-roots-media` block) only once the identification is established; an unidentified sitter stays in the Hypothesis note.
- Scans of documents found together with photos (extracts, certificates, ID cards) are documents, not photos: they go through `add-archive-record` with their own document `source_type`.

## 5. Privacy

- Collect further photos of living people, and include them in the vault, only to the extent the task needs and with the user's permission. Process family material the user provided locally without asking again.
- Do not upload family photos to third-party services (enhancement, colourisation, recognition) without the user's explicit permission.
- Check the rights to the picture and the source's terms: the page author is not necessarily the rights holder, and availability online does not mean permission to reproduce or publish it, including in a family report.

## 6. If nothing turns up

- The photo may not have survived or may be inaccessible. A `not-found` outcome means "not found in the sources checked", not "no photograph ever existed"; once the suitable sources are exhausted, close this item and continue with the other branches of the research.
- Record the sources checked in the `Survey -` note (nil sweep → `result: negative`). Leave missing family material in the open questions of the `Search Plan -` note, without repeating requests or waiting for an answer to continue.
